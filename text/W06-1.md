# The English Club

To practice English, Minh joined an online English club. Every Sunday evening, students from many countries met on a video call. It was the first time Minh talked to people from all over the world.

There was Yuki from Japan, who spoke Japanese and was learning English and Chinese. There was Carlos from Mexico, who spoke Spanish, and Ana from Brazil, who was Brazilian and spoke Portuguese. There was an Indian boy from India and a Thai girl from Thailand. There was a Korean student from Korea, Ji-woo, who loved Korean music, and an Italian girl from Italy, Giulia, who talked about Italian food all the time. Carlos said Mexican food was better. Yuki had a friend in China.

The teacher of the club was a **Canadian** woman named Sarah. She lived in Canada, but she was born in England. Her mother was British, from Britain, and her father was French. She also spoke German, a little Russian and a little Chinese. "I am a mix of many countries," she said.

"Where do you want to go, Minh?" Sarah asked in the first meeting.

"England," Minh said. "Or Australia. I want to study in an English-speaking country. I like British English. And maybe one day, I want to visit Germany, Spain and France too."

"Good choice," Sarah said. "Many Vietnamese students go to England or Australia. I have some Australian friends. They say the Australian accent is the best. But my British mother says the British accent is the best!"

Every week, they played games to learn English. One game was about colors. Sarah showed a picture and they had to say every color they could see: red, blue, yellow, black, white, brown, pink, purple, gray or grey (both are fine, Sarah said), gold and silver. Another game was about shapes. "A circle is round. A line is straight. What shape is the moon?"

"Round!" said Carlos. "But sometimes it is a little line."

Another game was about numbers. "Count from one to a hundred as fast as you can!" Minh came first. Yuki came second. Ji-woo came third. Carlos came last, but he did not care. "In Mexico, we are never in a hurry," he said.

They also learned words like many, much, more, most, some, any, all, every, few, little and a lot. "How many brothers do you have?" "How much time do you need?" "Do you have any questions?" Minh wrote them all in his notebook.

Sarah gave them some advice. "Each language is a new door," she said. "Some of you will travel to other countries. Both your languages, your own and English, are important. Never forget your own language."

After a few months, Minh had friends all over the world. He wrote messages to Yuki in Japan and Carlos in Mexico. He learned a lot about other cultures. In Japan, people take off their shoes at home, like in Vietnam. In Mexico, people eat late at night. In Brazil, everybody loves football, even more than in Vietnam. In Russia, it is very, very cold in winter.

"Vietnam is a small country," Minh told his mother one evening. "But the world is very big. And it is full of new people."

"Don't forget to come back," his mother said.

Minh laughed. "Mum, I haven't even left yet."

But in his heart, he was already a little bit far away.
