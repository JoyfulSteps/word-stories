# Designing a Wedding

The wedding planning started immediately. Mia, who was staying in Tokyo all summer, made herself the official designer. "You can't trust Minh with design," she told Hana. "He wears the same shirt every day."

"That's not true," said Minh. "I have three shirts."

First, the invitations. Mia wanted something special. She made a sample on thick, **matte** paper, not **glossy**. "Glossy paper looks cheap," she said. On the front, there was a **circular** design: two rivers meeting in a perfect circle. Around the edge, there was an **intricate** pattern of cherry flowers and lotus flowers. "Japan and Vietnam," she said. "And a small English rose in the middle."

Hana loved it. "It's beautiful. But it's very **ornate**. Maybe simpler?"

Mia made a second version. Simpler, cleaner, **symmetrical**: exactly the same on both sides. Then a third version, **asymmetrical**, with the rivers coming from different sides. They chose the third. "Life isn't symmetrical," said Hana.

Then the decorations. Mia wanted lanterns, like at the Obon festival. She bought paper lanterns in different shapes: **spherical** like balls, **cylindrical** like tubes, and **rectangular** like boxes. Some were **translucent**, so light came through softly. Others were **opaque**, with no light through them, but painted with gold. "The mix is important," she said. "Light and dark. Like a painting."

For the tables, she found old Japanese plates with a **hexagon** shape, six sides. The cake would be on a small **octagon** table, eight sides. "Every shape is a different **polygon**," said Lan, who had been helping. "Did you know that?"

"I'm an artist," said Mia. "I know shapes."

They visited a jewelry maker to make wedding rings. Hana already had the jade ring for the engagement. For the wedding, they wanted simple rings. The jeweler showed them different metals. Some were bright and **metallic**. Others were softer. One was **iridescent**, changing color in the light, like the inside of a shell.

"Gold is very **malleable**," the jeweler explained. "You can shape it easily. Silver is a little harder. Some metals are **brittle**; they break if you bend them. A ring must be strong."

He showed them how he made a ring. First, he drew the **contour** of the design on paper. Then he melted the metal and poured it into a mold. Then he polished it until its **texture** was perfect: smooth, not **coarse**. The finished rings were simple and beautiful.

Minh's ring was a little different. Inside, the jeweler engraved a tiny **elliptical** shape: an oval, like a river stone. "For your two rivers," the jeweler said. He had read Minh's book.

Then there was the dress. Hana wanted to wear a traditional Japanese kimono for the Japan ceremony, a Vietnamese áo dài for the Vietnam ceremony, and a simple white dress for England. "Three dresses for three weddings," she said. "My mother will be happy. My bank account won't."

Mia designed the English dress. It was simple but elegant: soft silk, with a small **triangular** pattern on the back. "Grandma Rose will make it," said Mia. "She insists."

"She's eighty-five," said Hana.

"She says she'll make it if it's the last thing she does," said Mia. "Which, she says, it won't be, because she wants to see your children first."

Hana went red. Minh laughed.

The last thing was the location in England. Grandma Rose offered Margaret's cottage in Scotland. "By the river," she said on a video call. "Where else? Margaret would want it."

They looked at photos of the cottage again. The stone walls were **porous**, old stone that held water, so the house was always a little damp. The garden was **compact**, small but beautiful. In spring, it would be full of flowers.

"It's perfect," said Hana. "A wedding by a river."

"Our friend from Mexico at the party said a **Hispanic** wedding lasts all night," said Lan. "With dancing until morning."

"Three weddings," said Minh. "Three rivers. Tokyo, Hanoi, Scotland."

"Your life is a book," said Mia. "You know that, right?"

"I know," said Minh. "I'm writing it."

The rope that held the lanterns at the engagement party had been **elastic**, stretching and stretching. Like his life, Minh thought. It stretched to fit everything.
