# Christmas Day

On Christmas morning, Minh woke up early. The house was quiet. Then he heard a scream from downstairs. It was Mia. "He came! Santa came!"

Mia was fifteen. She did not believe in Santa. But on Christmas morning, she pretended.

Everyone came down in their pajamas. Under the tree, there were presents for everyone. There was even a big pile for Minh. He was surprised. "For me?"

"Of course," said Anna. "You're family."

Minh opened his presents. A warm hat from Grandma Rose. A book about British history from Anna. A Riverford football shirt from Leo. A drawing of the house from Mia. And from Tom, a big jar of **peanut** butter and a note: "The most American thing I could find."

Breakfast was a feast. Tom made **toast**, eggs and bacon. There was **cereal** for the children, **yogurt** with **honey** for Anna, and Grandma Rose's homemade strawberry **jam**. There were warm bread **rolls**. Tom put **ketchup** on everything. Grandma Rose put **mustard** on her sausages. Minh tried **mayonnaise** with his eggs. "That's disgusting," said Leo. "That's French," said Grandma Rose.

Then the cooking began. Christmas dinner was the biggest meal of the year. Grandma Rose was the chef. Everyone else was her helper.

She showed Minh how to make the stuffing for the turkey, with bread, onions and herbs. She showed him how to put **oil**, salt and **pepper** on the potatoes before they went in the oven. They made a **sauce** for the turkey, and a special bread sauce that Minh didn't like at all.

For the first course, there was smoked **salmon** on brown bread. Some people had a small **tuna** salad instead. Then came the turkey, the roast potatoes, the vegetables and the gravy. Minh had never eaten so much in his life.

Then came the **desserts**. There was a Christmas **pudding**, a dark, heavy cake with fruit. Tom poured brandy on it and lit it with a match. Blue fire covered the pudding for a few seconds. Everyone clapped. There was also an apple **pie**, made with Grandma Rose's own **flour** and butter, and a small lemon **tart** that Mia had made.

"I can't eat any more," said Minh.

"Nonsense," said Grandma Rose. "There's always room for pudding."

In the afternoon, they watched films. Leo made **popcorn**. Mia ate a whole bag of **crisps**. "In America, we call those **chips**," said Tom. "And chips are what we call **fries**." Nobody listened.

There was a bowl of **nuts** on the table: walnuts, **almonds**, and a big **nut** that was so hard nobody could open it. Tom tried for ten minutes. Then Grandma Rose took it, hit it once with a hammer, and gave it back to him. Everyone laughed.

In the evening, Minh called his family in Vietnam. It was early morning there. His mother answered, half asleep. "Merry Christmas, Mum," he said in English.

"Merry Christmas," said his mother, in her careful English. Then, in Vietnamese: "Are you eating enough?"

Minh looked at the table, still covered with food. "Yes, Mum," he said. "I'm eating enough."

He showed her the house on the video call: the tree, the presents, the family. Everyone waved. Grandma Rose came close to the camera. "Hello! Your son is a good boy!" she shouted. Minh's mother didn't understand the words, but she understood the smile. She smiled back.

That night, Minh lay in bed, too full to sleep. It was his first Christmas. It wouldn't be his last. He thought about his two families: one in Vietnam, one here. He was lucky. Very lucky.
