# The Storm and the Sanctuary

In March, a terrible storm hit Riverford. The wind was so strong that it tore roofs off houses and pulled trees from the ground. It lasted one night. In the **aftermath**, the town woke up to **debris** everywhere: broken branches, pieces of roof, a car upside down in the park.

Minh, now acting editor, organised the coverage. He sent reporters to every part of town. He went himself to the worst area: the farms by the river.

The damage was huge. A barn full of **hay** had collapsed. Fences were down. Animals were **scattered** across the fields. Leo was there, helping. He was working for a vet practice now. "We've found forty sheep so far," he said. "And three horses. One is injured."

Leo had a plan. Near the river, there was an old farm that had become an animal **sanctuary**, a safe place for rescued animals. He brought the injured horse there. The sanctuary was run by a woman called Ruth, who had dedicated her life to animals. Her **dedication** was famous: she had worked seven days a week for twenty years.

"Animals are **prone** to panic in storms," said Ruth. "They run, they get hurt. We need to catch them quickly, with **caution**. Approach slowly. Don't frighten them more."

Leo worked through the day. He was calm and skilled. Minh watched his cousin, as he thought of him now, with pride. Leo had a strong **grasp** of animal behaviour. He knew how to **dodge** a frightened horse's kick. He knew which animals needed treatment and which needed rest.

Over the next weeks, the town began **reconstruction**. Builders fixed roofs. Volunteers cleared the park. The council asked the national government for emergency money. Minh's newspaper published a list of everyone who needed help and everyone who could give it. The response was amazing.

Some problems **arose**. A few builders charged too much, taking advantage of the crisis. One man was arrested for stealing from damaged houses. Some insurance companies were slow to pay. Minh's reporters covered every story.

The storm also damaged the new high-rise building by the river, still under construction. Its **architectural** design had been praised by experts; it was a **nominee** for a national prize. Now some of its glass panels were broken. The **renowned** architect came from London to see. "The structure is fine," she said. "It did exactly what it should. The building moved with the wind. Only the glass broke. That's easy to fix."

The architects' **guild** later gave the building a special award for its storm resistance. It was the first **recipient** of the award in the region. Minh wrote about it as a **showcase** of good design.

There was a **spike** in hospital visits after the storm: cuts, falls, a few broken bones. Hana worked extra shifts. Most patients were **discharged** quickly. One old man had been hit by a falling tree branch while trying to save his **automobile**, a 1965 classic car. "The car survived," he said proudly from his hospital bed. "So did I. Just about."

Leo got a **certification** from the vet practice for his emergency work. His supervisor wrote: "Under **supervision**, Leo showed exceptional skill and calm." It was a **determining** moment in his career. A month later, he was offered a full-time job at the sanctuary.

"Is it a **leap** of faith?" Minh asked him.

"Totally," said Leo. "The pay is terrible. But I love it."

The sanctuary had a **catalogue** of all its animals: thirty dogs, twenty cats, ten horses, sheep, goats, a donkey called Mr. Brown (from Gareth's farm in Wales; he had retired there) and a very **territorial** goose who attacked everyone. Leo loved them all, even the goose.

Minh published a special edition about the storm: a batch of stories, photos and interviews. It was the Gazette's best-selling edition in ten years. He decided to execute a new plan from then on: every big event would have a special edition, combining print, website and podcast.

Grace, who was still in the office for a few more weeks, read it. "Good," she said. "Very good." Then she added, "Too many photos of Leo."

"He was the hero," said Minh.

"Fair enough," said Grace.
