# The Engagement Party

When Minh came back from Vietnam, there was big news. Grandma Rose's niece Kate, the nurse, was **engaged**. Her boyfriend, Ben, had asked her to marry him on Valentine's Day. "On one knee, in the rain, in front of the hospital," Kate said. "Very romantic and very wet."

There was an **engagement** party at the Parkers' house. Anna was the **hostess**. She cooked for two days. The house was full of people of every generation: babies, children, **teenage** cousins, young couples and **elderly** relatives.

Minh brought Hana. It was the first time she came to the house. He was nervous. He made a short **introduction**: "Everyone, this is Hana. She's a friend from college." Leo said, "A friend? Just a friend?" Minh wanted to kill him.

Hana was a little shy at first, but soon she was talking to everyone. Grandma Rose liked her immediately. "She's a nurse? Good. A nurse is useful. Marry her."

"Grandma!" said Minh. His face was on fire.

Kate and Ben told everyone about their plans. The wedding was in June. Kate would be the **bride**, of course, and Ben the **groom**. They wanted a small wedding by the river and a **honeymoon** in Greece. Kate showed everyone her ring.

Grandma Rose talked about her own marriage. "Fifty years with your grandfather," she said to Tom. "We had good times and hard times. But we never thought about **divorce**. Not once. Well, maybe once. In 1974."

Everyone laughed. One of the elderly aunts said she was **divorced** twice and happier now than ever. Another said her husband was finally enjoying his **retirement**. "He's retired, and I'm tired," she said. "He's at home all day now."

Ben's sister was **pregnant**. Her baby was coming in May. Her husband talked about the future. "We want two children," he said. "And maybe one day we will **adopt** a child too. There are so many children who need a home."

A teenage boy, Ben's cousin, was very quiet. He was Hana's **flatmate**'s brother, it turned out. The world was small. Ben's own roommate at university was there too, telling embarrassing stories about Ben's **youth**. "He couldn't cook anything. He ate cereal for two years."

Not everyone at the party was happy. An old uncle of Ben's had just come from a **funeral**. His oldest friend had died. "That's the strange thing about life," he said quietly to Minh. "A funeral in the morning and a party in the evening. Sad and happy in one day."

Minh thought about his childhood in Vietnam, his grandmother, the old men who told stories. Some of them were gone now. "That's how life is," he said. "Everything together."

Later, Minh found Hana in the corner of the living room with a book. "Are you studying? At a party?"

"Sorry," she said. "I have an anatomy exam on Monday. I have to learn every bone and every **organ** in the body."

"Let me help," said Minh. "Ask me."

She opened the book. "What's this bone?" She pointed to a picture of the head.

"The **skull**," said Minh. "Easy."

"And this?" She touched her face, under her mouth.

"The **jaw**."

"Good. And these?" She pointed to the long bones around her chest.

"The **ribs**. And the **spine** in the back. I know these from Grandma Rose's yoga."

"Now the organs. This is the **liver**. And these two, at the back, are the **kidneys**."

Then Leo came in. He made a **fist** and pretended to hit Minh on the arm. "Are you two studying bones at a party? You're the most boring couple in the world."

"We're not a couple," said Minh and Hana at the same time. Then they looked at each other and laughed.

The owner of the house next door complained about the noise at eleven o'clock. Anna apologized. Kate and Ben danced in the kitchen. The relationship between them, Minh thought, was easy and warm. They seemed to fit together.

When the party was over, Minh walked Hana home. It was cold and quiet. "Your English family is wonderful," she said. "I felt very welcome."

"They liked you," said Minh. "Especially Grandma Rose."

"She told me to marry you," Hana said, smiling.

"She tells everyone to marry everyone," said Minh quickly.

"I know," said Hana. "But it was still nice to hear."

Minh did not know what to say. So he said nothing, and they walked on, side by side, under the stars.
