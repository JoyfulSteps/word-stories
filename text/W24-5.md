# The Green Café

After the flood and the plant sale, Daniel had a new idea. "If we save The Blue Cup," he said, "I want it to be better than before. Greener. More sustainable."

He asked Ffion for advice. She came from the university with a list. "First, the carbon **footprint** of your café," she said. "How much energy do you use? How much waste do you make? How much consumption of water?"

"A lot," said Daniel honestly. "We consume a lot of everything."

Ffion explained. "Most energy in Britain still comes from **fossil** fuels: coal, oil and gas. They make **emissions** of carbon dioxide. That's the main cause of climate change. Renewable energy, like solar panels and wind **turbines**, is much better."

"Can a small café put solar panels on its roof?"

"Yes, if the owner agrees. And there are other things. Use less plastic. Reuse cups. Compost your food waste instead of sending it to landfill."

Minh listened carefully. He was writing an article about it for the university newspaper. Since the flood, he had become very interested in environmental problems.

In Vietnam, he told Ffion, the problems were bigger. "In Hanoi, the air is very **polluted**. There's a lot of **smog** from motorbikes and factories. Some days, you can't see the sky. And in the countryside, there's deforestation: people cut down the forests to make farms."

"It's a global problem," said Ffion. "Factories **pollute** rivers with toxic chemicals. **Sewage** goes into the sea. The ozone layer was damaged by chemicals in the past. Natural disasters are getting worse because of climate change. Like your flood."

"Do you think the flood was because of climate change?" asked Daniel.

"Probably partly. Floods like this are more common now."

Daniel thought for a long time. "OK," he said. "When we reopen, we go green. No plastic straws. No plastic cups. A **conservation** box for coffee grounds. Local food only."

They started immediately. Mia painted a sign: "The Green Cup: Save Energy, Save the Planet." Leo made a compost box for the garden behind the café. Grandma Rose became the **gardener**, of course. She showed Minh how to **prune** the old roses so they would grow better, and how to **sow** seeds in spring.

"Plants teach you patience," she said. "You **sow** the seeds. They **sprout**. They grow. The fruit **ripens** slowly. If you leave it too long, it **rots**. If you forget to water it, it **withers**. Life is like a garden."

Ffion wrote an article for the university **ecological** society about the café. Minh wrote one for the newspaper. People started to talk about The Blue Cup again. "The green café that wants to survive," said the headline.

A week later, Daniel got an email. It was from a company that made solar panels. "We read about you," the email said. "We'd like to give you free solar panels, if you stay open. It would be good for our image."

Daniel read it three times. "If we stay open," he said. "That's the problem."

"Then we have to stay open," said Minh. "We have to preserve this place. Conserve it. Like an endangered species."

Ffion laughed. "An endangered café. I like that."

That night, Minh looked up at the stars from his flat in the city. He thought about how small the earth was in the universe, and how much we needed to protect it. He thought about all the resources we used without thinking. He thought about The Blue Cup. Small things mattered. A small café, a small garden, a small change. If enough people made small changes, maybe the world could change too.

He opened his laptop and started a new article. It was about **sustainability**, but really, it was about hope.
