# Hana's Patient

In July, Hana worked on a special case. A young man, twenty-three, needed a kidney **transplantation**. He had been waiting for two years. Finally, a donor was found: his own sister.

Hana was the nurse responsible for both of them. She came on the podcast to talk about it, with their permission.

"Transplant surgery is amazing," she said. "Taking an organ from one person and putting it in another. It's one of the greatest achievements of human **ingenuity**."

She explained the process. "First, many tests. The organ must match. Then the operation: the surgeons remove the kidney from the donor and place it in the patient. Then careful monitoring. The body can reject the new organ. We give medicine to stop that."

The operation was a success. The young man, Daniel Shaw (no relation to The Blue Cup's Daniel), recovered well. His sister was fine too. "She gave me life," he said. "**Reciprocal** love. Now I owe her everything."

Hana also talked about **psychotherapy**: talking therapy for mental health. "Many transplant patients need it," she said. "It's a big emotional experience. They feel grateful, but also guilty. Talking helps."

The episode was popular. Many listeners signed up to be organ donors. "That's the best result I could hope for," said Hana.

There were other stories that month.

The town held its first **kayak** race on the river. A hundred people took part, from children to grandparents. Leo, as director of the river trust, gave the prizes. The winner was a sixty-year-old woman who had learned to kayak only last year. She paddled **gracefully** and fast. "Age is just a number," she said.

The museum opened an exhibition of old **tapestries**: large pictures woven from wool and silk. One showed the town in 1650. Helen Ward explained: "Tapestries were the films of their time. They told stories."

The council voted to **dismantle** the old 1970s car park, the ugly concrete building in the centre. It would become a green space. Some people protested. Most were happy.

The national government announced a new **guideline** for schools on mental health. Lan, now teaching in Tokyo, joined the podcast to compare Japan and Britain. "Japanese schools are starting to talk about mental health too," she said. "But it's **embryonic**. Very new."

Leo, at the river trust, had a hard decision. A rich landowner had been **hoarding** water from a stream for his private lake. The stream was drying up. Leo confronted him. The landowner was **forceful** and angry. Leo was calm. "It's not your water," he said. "It belongs to the river." The landowner eventually agreed to share.

Helen Ward told a story on the podcast about **stewardship**: the idea that we are not owners of the land, but its caretakers. "Old cultures understood this," she said. "We're slowly learning it again."

She also talked about a dark period in history: the **annihilation** of whole communities during wars. "History has many **calamities**," she said. "We study them so we don't repeat them."

A computer problem at the council **crippled** the town's website for a week. Some **erroneous** information was published: wrong dates for bin collections, wrong addresses. "**Infiltration** by hackers?" asked Minh. "No," said the council. "Just an old system." The question of the website became **moot** when the council bought a new one.

Minh's own life **revolved** around family and work. Rose Mai was nearly three. She spoke three languages, mixing them all in one sentence. "**Vernacular** of a small international family," said Hana, laughing. "She'll be a linguist."

The month **culminated** in a big family party: Grandma Rose's ninety-third birthday. She was weaker now, but her mind was sharp. She gave a short speech. "I've lived a **virtuous** life," she said. "Mostly. Some mistakes. But I've loved well. That's enough."

A **sizeable** cake was made by Daniel. Rose Mai helped blow out the candles. The **fragmentation** of the family across countries was forgotten for one evening. They were all together, at least by video. It was an **uninterrupted** evening of happiness. Like a picture laid as an **overlay** on all the hard times, a beautiful layer that would **shatter** any sadness for a while. Minh thought it was the best evening in years. **Wrongdoing**, hardship, loss: none of it existed for those few hours.
