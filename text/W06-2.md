# The Interview

In the spring of his last year at school, Minh got an email. It said: "Dear Minh, thank you for your application. We would like to invite you to an interview for the scholarship." Minh read it three times. Then he ran downstairs to show his mother.

"An interview! With whom?" she asked.

"With a teacher from the college in England. On a video call. Next Friday."

For a week, Minh practiced every day. He wanted to do it all by himself, but Mr. Jake helped him. "These are the questions that they usually ask," he said, and gave Minh a list. "Learn them, or practice them with another person." "They will ask about you, your family, your school and your plans," he said. "Speak slowly. Smile. And if you don't understand a question, ask them to say it again."

On Friday, Minh sat at his desk in front of the computer. His mother was in the kitchen. His father was at his shop, but he called three times to say, "Good luck." He turned off his phone. Lan was told to stay quiet. She sat outside the door with her ear on it.

The interview started. On the screen, there was a woman with short gray hair and kind eyes. "Hello, Minh. My name is Dr. Wilson. How are you?"

"I'm fine, thank you. And you?"

"Very well. Tell me about yourself."

Minh took a deep breath. "My name is Minh. I am from a small town in the north of Vietnam. I live with my parents, my sister and my grandmother. My father fixes motorbikes, and my mother is a teacher. They work very hard for us. Their dream is for my sister and me to have a good education."

"Which subject do you want to study?"

"English and business, if I can."

"Why do you want to study in England?"

"Because I love English, and I want to learn about the world. If I study in England, I can learn about other people and other ideas. When I finish, I want to come back and help my town."

"What do you do in your free time?"

"I play the guitar in a band with my friends. Our band is very bad, but we have fun. I also go to an English club online. It has students from many countries."

Dr. Wilson smiled. "And what is the most difficult thing about English, for you?"

Minh thought. "The small words," he said. "In, on, at, to, from, by, about, over, under, between... In Vietnamese, it is different. I always say 'in Monday' and not 'on Monday.' And 'he' and 'she' and 'it'... In Vietnamese we don't use them the same way. I mix up him and her, and his and hers."

Dr. Wilson laughed. "Count yourself lucky. Those words are difficult for everybody. Even English children make mistakes with them."

Then she asked some harder questions. Why this college? Where do you see yourself in five years? What is a problem in your town, and how can you help with it? Minh did his best. Sometimes he did not know a word, so he explained it with other words. Once, he did not understand a question, so he asked her to say it again, like Mr. Jake said.

At the end, she said, "Thank you, Minh. We will write to you in two months. Do you have any questions for us?"

"Yes," Minh said. "Whose idea was the scholarship? And what is the town like? Is the college famous for its English classes? Is it near the sea?"

"It's a small town by a river, with an old castle on a hill. It's quiet and green. I think you'll like it. If you come."

After the call, Minh sat still for a long time. Then he opened the door. Lan fell into the room. "Did you hear everything?" he asked.

"Yes," she said. "You said 'by a river' and 'on Monday.' Mr. Jake would be proud of you."

Minh laughed. Then he went to tell his mother, who was waiting behind the kitchen door, and his grandmother, who was praying for him by the window, between her two favorite plants. Without them, he thought, none of this was possible. "This is not mine," he said to his mother. "It's yours and Dad's and Grandma's. It's ours. I didn't do it myself. When Lan and Grandma ask whose dream this is, I will say it is theirs too." Up and down, in and out, his heart did not stop all evening.
