# Students of the World

At the graduation party, Minh's parents met students from all over the world. His mother kept a list in her notebook. "So many countries," she said. "I want to remember them all."

There was a girl from **Finland**, who spoke **Finnish** and wore no coat even when it was cold. "In Finland, this is summer," she said. There was a boy from **Denmark** who taught Minh's father to say "cheers" in **Danish**. There was a **Belgian** girl from **Belgium** who brought chocolate for everyone, and an **Austrian** student from **Austria** who played the piano at the party.

There was a quiet **Hungarian** student from **Hungary** who studied engineering, and a **Czech** student who said **Czech** beer was the best in the world. There was a **Ukrainian** student from **Ukraine** who had come to England as a refugee during the war. She studied medicine, like Hana. "One day I'll go home and help rebuild my country," she said.

From the Middle East, there was a **Saudi** student, very polite, and an **Iraqi** student from **Iraq** who studied architecture. There was a young man from **Afghanistan** who had walked across five countries to reach England when he was fifteen. Minh's mother listened to his story and held his hand. She did not understand the words, but she understood.

There were students from Asia, too. A girl from **Bangladesh** who wanted to be a doctor. A boy from **Myanmar** who studied business with Minh. A **Mongolian** student from **Mongolia** who said his family lived in a tent on the grassland and had three hundred horses. "Like a cowboy," said Leo, impressed. A student from **Nepal**, who had climbed half of Everest with his father, a mountain guide.

And there were students from the Caribbean and Africa. A **Cuban** student from **Cuba** taught everyone to dance salsa. A **Jamaican** student from **Jamaica** played music on a big speaker. Minh's father tried to dance. Everyone laughed, including him.

Minh's mother asked Minh to show her where all these countries were. So he took out his phone and opened a map. He showed her **Antarctica** at the bottom of the world, covered in ice. He showed her the **Arctic** at the top. "Penguins live in Antarctica," he said. "Polar bears in the Arctic. They never meet."

He showed her the oceans. The **Atlantic**, between Europe and America. The **Pacific**, the biggest ocean, between Asia and America. "Vietnam is on the Pacific," he said. "England is on the Atlantic. That's why it's so far."

"And the **Mediterranean**?" asked Sam, who was listening. "That's my sea. In Spain. The most beautiful sea in the world."

"Everyone says their sea is the most beautiful," said Hana.

Next to the Mediterranean, Minh showed his mother **Morocco**, the African country where Sam almost went last summer. "Next time," Sam said again.

Minh's father was amazed. "When I was young, I knew only Vietnam, China, Russia, America and France," he said. "Because of the wars. Now my son has friends from fifty countries."

"Is that good?" asked Minh's mother.

Minh's father thought. "Yes," he said. "Very good. If everyone had friends from fifty countries, there would be no more wars."

At the end of the party, all the students stood together for a photo. Fifty faces from fifty countries, all smiling. Minh's mother took the photo with her phone. "This is going on my wall at home," she said. "Next to the photo of Ho Chi Minh and the photo of your grandfather."

"That's a big honor," said Minh.

"It's a big photo," said his mother. "Many people in it."
