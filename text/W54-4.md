# Tom's Heart

In December, Tom had a heart attack.

It happened at home, on a Sunday morning. He was carrying firewood. Suddenly, he felt a terrible pain in his chest. He sat down. His face went gray. Anna called an ambulance. Grandma Rose, ninety-two, held his hand and didn't let go.

At the hospital, the doctors acted quickly. It was a **coronary** event: one of the arteries that carry blood to the heart was blocked. They opened it with a small tube. Tom survived.

Hana was working that day. She saw Tom brought in. She stayed with the family the whole time. "He's in good hands," she said. "The team here is the best."

Minh came immediately with Rose Mai. Leo drove from the sanctuary. Mia took the first train from London. By evening, the whole family was in the waiting room.

When Tom woke up, the first thing he said was: "Did anyone feed the cat?"

Everyone laughed and cried at the same time.

Tom's recovery took months. He had to change his life. No more fried breakfasts, no more pizza every Friday. Exercise every day. Less stress. The doctor was **proactive**: she made a plan with him for a whole year.

"What was the **culprit**?" asked Minh, when Tom was well enough to talk. "Why did it happen?"

"Too much of everything," said Tom. "Too much food, too much work, not enough exercise. And my father had heart problems too. It's in the family."

He came on the podcast a few months later, with his doctor. They talked about heart health.

"Heart disease is the biggest killer in Britain," said the doctor. "But much of it is preventable. Healthy food, exercise, not smoking. **Irrespective** of your age, you can make changes."

She talked about a long study of a **cohort** of men and women followed for thirty years. "The results are clear. People who walk every day live longer. People who eat vegetables live longer. It's not complicated. But it's **daunting** for many people to change habits."

"What's the biggest **hurdle**?" asked Minh.

"Motivation," said the doctor. "People wait until something bad happens. Like Tom."

"I'm a lesson for everyone," said Tom. "Learn from me. Don't wait."

She also talked about **disparity** in heart health: poor people had more heart disease than rich people. "Poverty, stress, bad food, less access to doctors. We need **parity**, equality, in health care. Right now, there's a big gap."

There were other stories that month.

The council debated a plan to **divert** traffic away from the old town. Some shop owners worried it would hurt business. In the end, the vote was decided by a **plurality**: the plan with the most votes won, though not a majority. The mayor tried to **override** the decision. The council said no. Minh covered the story.

Helen Ward talked about the history of empires and **annexation**: when one country takes another country's land. "It has happened throughout history," she said. "**Chiefly** by force. It's always unjust. And it always leads to resistance."

At the dig, Sophie found a small artifact: a bronze mirror, beautifully **carved**. "It belonged to someone rich," she said. "Someone who wanted to see her own face, two thousand years ago. Human nature doesn't change."

The sanctuary had an unwelcome **intrusion**: a fox got into the hen house at night and killed six chickens. Leo was upset. "Nature is nature," he said. "But I'll build a better fence."

A **reputable** university in London invited Minh to teach a short course on local journalism. He accepted. "I'm not a professor," he said. "Just a reporter who learned by doing."

Minh's own health became a question too. After Tom's heart attack, Hana made him get a checkup. "You're thirty-two, you work too much, and you eat too much cake at The Blue Cup," she said. The results were fine, but the doctor said he needed to exercise more. Minh started running by the river every morning. He was very slow. Once, he **tumbled** over a tree root and fell into a bush. Rose Mai thought it was hilarious when he came home covered in leaves.

At Christmas, the whole family gathered at the Parkers' house. Tom was much better. He was thinner and walked every day. He made a short speech at dinner.

"This year, I nearly died," he said. "And it made me see what matters. Not work. Not money. You. All of you." He looked at each of them. "My mother, who held my hand. My wife, who called the ambulance. My children. My Vietnamese son and his family. Thank you."

Nobody said anything. Then Grandma Rose said, "And you didn't even need the pizza."

Everyone laughed. It was the best Christmas in years. Even in **solitude** later that night, washing dishes alone in the kitchen, Minh felt full of gratitude. The **expulsion** of fear from the house, after weeks of worry, felt like a new beginning. Tom could finally **defy** the doctors' worst predictions. He was **prominently** placed at the head of the table, where he belonged.

There was a lighter story too. Lan, now a teacher in Tokyo, sent Minh a funny message: one of her students had answered an arithmetic question with a long essay. "Maybe he'll be a journalist," she wrote. She also described how her school was **characterised** by its love of experiments. It sounded wonderful. A **basal** rule in her class: no question is stupid. The use of **filler** words like "um" was banned in her speaking class, though she admitted she used them herself. The **substitution** of a kind word for a harsh one, she said, changed everything in teaching. Minh agreed confidently. Some things, he thought, were true everywhere.
