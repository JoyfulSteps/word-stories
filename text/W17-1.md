# Mia's Painting

In October, Mia entered a national art competition. The theme was "One World." "I want to paint a picture with every country in it," she said. "Every flag, every color."

Minh helped her find information. They sat at the kitchen table with her laptop and looked at flags from all over the world.

"Look at **Israel**," said Mia. "White and blue, with a star. And **Iran** has green, white and red." She asked Minh, "Do you know any **Israeli** or **Iranian** people?"

"There's an **Iranian** doctor at the hospital, Kate says," said Minh. "And an **Israeli** student in my class."

They looked at more flags. **Pakistan**: green with a white moon. **Nigeria**: green, white, green. **Kenya**: black, red and green, with a shield in the middle. "The **Kenyan** flag is my favourite," said Mia. "It's so strong."

At the English club, Minh had met a **Pakistani** boy and a **Nigerian** girl. The **Nigerian** girl loved music. The **Pakistani** boy loved cricket and talked about it all the time.

Then they looked at South America. **Peru** had red and white stripes. **Chile** had a white star on blue. **Colombia** was yellow, blue and red. "In the English club, there's a **Peruvian** girl, a **Chilean** man and a **Colombian** boy," said Minh. "They argue about football all the time."

Mia started to paint. Her painting was huge, as big as a door. In the middle, there was the earth, round and blue. Around it, there were hands of every color, holding each other. And around the hands, there were flags, small and **colorful**.

"Every flag is a different shape," she said. "Most are a rectangle. But look at Nepal. It's two triangles." She painted some flags as **ovals**, to make the picture more interesting.

Minh watched her work. She used a thick brush for the big areas and a very narrow one for the details. In one place, she painted tiny dots. In another place, she painted **stripes**, like the American flag. Her T-shirt was **striped** too, black and white, and covered in paint.

"How do you make the sky look so deep?" Minh asked.

"I use many layers," said Mia. "Dark blue, then lighter blue, then a **pale** blue on top. And see this **spot**? I leave it white, for light."

She showed him a **pattern** in the hands: small lines, like fingerprints. "Every person is different," she said. "Every hand has its own pattern."

Some parts of the painting were **smooth**, like glass. Other parts were **rough**, with thick paint you could touch. The paper at the edges was wide and white, like a frame.

After three weeks, the painting was finished. The whole family stood in front of it. Nobody said anything for a minute.

"Mia," said Anna finally, "it's beautiful."

"It's very good," said Tom. "Honestly. I'm not just saying that because I'm your father."

Grandma Rose looked at it for a long time. "In my day," she said quietly, "the world was at war. Countries hated each other. This picture is what we hoped for." She touched the frame. "You have a gift, Mia."

Mia's face went red. She sent the painting to the competition. Now she had to wait two months for the result. "Waiting is the worst part," she said.

"I know," said Minh. He remembered waiting for his scholarship. "But good things are worth waiting for."
