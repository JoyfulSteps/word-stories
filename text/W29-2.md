# The Gig

In March, The Blue Cup finally reopened after the flood repairs. Daniel wanted a big celebration. "A night of music," he said. "Everyone who can play, plays."

Daniel's own band would play, of course. Daniel was the **guitarist** and the **vocalist**. His friend Mike was the **drummer**. A new member, a young woman called Priya, was a **pianist**. And there was a **violinist**, an old man who used to play in an orchestra.

"I want you to write about it," Grace told Minh. "From the inside. The **rehearsals**, the preparations, everything."

So Minh went to every **rehearsal**. They **rehearsed** in the café after closing time, three nights a week. It was not easy. The drummer was always too loud. The pianist was always too fast. The violinist was always late. Daniel shouted a lot.

"Is it always like this?" Minh asked Priya.

"Always," she said. "Musicians are **enthusiasts**. We all love music so much that we argue about every note."

Priya was also a **composer**. She **composed** her own songs. She played one for Minh, a beautiful, sad piece. "It's about my grandmother," she said. "She came from India in 1970." Minh asked about her **composition**. "I write the melody first," she said. "Then the **harmony**. Then the words."

The old violinist had been a **soloist** in a big orchestra in London. He had played with a famous **conductor**. "I played on the **soundtrack** for three films," he said proudly. "One was a hit. It went to number one in the chart." He had a recording of it on his phone. He played it for everyone at least five times.

On the night of the **gig**, the café was full. People stood outside in the street, looking through the windows. There were families, students, old people. Everyone who had bought a share in the café was there.

The band played for two hours. They played old songs and new songs, fast songs and slow songs. At first, they were nervous. Then they relaxed. The music was warm and **amusing**. Daniel told funny stories between songs. People laughed and sang along.

Halfway through, Daniel stopped. "I want to say thank you," he said. "A year ago, this café almost closed. Now it belongs to you. All of you. So this song is for you."

They played a song that Priya had written about The Blue Cup. It was about a small café by a river, and the people who loved it. Grandma Rose cried. Pat cried. Even Daniel cried a little.

Minh looked around the room. Everyone was different. There were **gamers** who usually spent all night online. There were **birdwatching** **hobbyists** from the nature club. There was a woman who made a **scrapbook** of every event in Riverford. There was a man who called himself a coffee **addict** and drank five cups a day. And there were Hana, Sam, Mia, Leo, Tom and Anna. Music made them all one.

After the concert, people stayed to talk and **socialize**. Leo talked to the birdwatchers about rare birds. Mia talked to Priya about art and music. Sam talked to everyone about his new company. Hana and Minh sat in the corner, holding hands.

"This is my favourite kind of **outing**," said Hana. "Not **clubbing**. Not expensive restaurants. Just friends and music."

"Mine too," said Minh.

Minh wrote his article that night. He wrote about **recreation** and community, about the power of music to **entertain**, to **amuse**, to bring people together. He wrote about **blogging** and **gaming**, the modern hobbies that keep people at home, and about **recreational** activities that bring them out. "We spend our lives alone with our screens," he wrote. "We need nights like this. Nights when we remember we're not alone."

Grace read it the next morning. "It's good," she said. "It's very good. It's a little bit sentimental."

"Is that bad?"

"Not this time," said Grace. "This time, it's right."
