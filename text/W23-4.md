# Around the World in One Evening

Minh's idea was simple. "Let's have an evening of travel stories," he said. "People pay ten pounds to come. Speakers talk about places they've been. All the money goes to the café."

Daniel was not sure. "Who wants to listen to holiday stories?"

"Everyone," said Minh. "If they're good stories."

So they organized it. They called it "Around the World in One Evening." They found six speakers. The café was not ready yet after the flood, so the church let them use its hall. They sold a hundred tickets in a week.

The first speaker was Ffion, the scientist from Wales. She talked about Iceland. She showed photos of a huge **glacier**, a river of ice moving slowly down a mountain. She showed an **iceberg** in a **bay**, white and blue. "Ninety percent of an iceberg is under the water," she said. "Only ten percent is on the surface."

The second speaker was Sam. He talked about Spain. He showed his family's village, on a dry **plateau** high above the sea, and the beautiful **scenery** of the south coast. "On a clear day, from the **summit** of the mountain, you can see Africa," he said. "Another **continent**, just across the sea."

The third speaker was an old customer, a retired teacher who had traveled everywhere. He talked about America. He showed photos of the Grand **Canyon**, deep and red. He talked about the wide **prairie** in the middle of the country, flat grass for thousands of kilometers. "In the old days, millions of buffalo lived on the prairie," he said. He also showed a **swamp** in Florida, full of alligators, and a **marsh** with tall birds. "Every **region** of America is different. It's not one country. It's fifty."

The fourth speaker was Hana. She was nervous, but she talked about Japan. She showed Mount Fuji, with snow on its **peak**, and the steep **slope** that people climb every summer. She talked about the **seasonal** changes: cherry flowers in spring, red leaves in autumn. "In June, we have the rainy season," she said. "Like a small **monsoon**."

Then Minh talked about Vietnam. He showed the long, thin shape of the country. "Vietnam is like an S. At the top is the north, where I'm from. In the south, there's the Mekong Delta, where the river meets the sea." He showed Ha Long **Bay**, with thousands of rock islands. He showed the **tropical** **rainforest** in the mountains, the beaches, and the green rice fields. He talked about the **Gulf** of Tonkin and the old French **canal** in Saigon. "Vietnam has a long coast. We live with the sea, the **tide** and the rain."

The last speaker was Gareth, Ffion's father. He talked about a trip to Australia. He showed a photo of the Great Barrier **Reef**, with colorful **coral** and fish. "The coral is dying because the water is getting too warm," he said seriously. He also showed a green **oasis** in the desert, like a dream in the middle of the sand, and a beautiful **meadow** of flowers in the mountains. He talked about an Australian **peninsula** and a **ridge** of red rock. "Australia is a huge **territory**," he said. "You can drive for three days and see nobody."

At the end, everybody clapped. People stayed for tea and cake. They talked about the places they wanted to see.

When they counted the money, they had raised two thousand pounds. "That's amazing," said Daniel. He looked tired, but happy. "Thank you. All of you."

"It's not enough," said Minh.

"No," said Daniel. "But it's something. Every pound helps."

On the way home, Hana held Minh's hand. "Where do you want to go most in the world?" she asked.

"Everywhere," said Minh. "But first, I want this café to stay open."

"Me too," said Hana. "Then everywhere. Together."
