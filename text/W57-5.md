# The Vote

In December, Riverford held a **plebiscite**, a vote by all residents, on a big question: should the town join with two neighbouring villages to form one larger council? Supporters said it would save money. Opponents said the villages would be **marginalised**, their voices lost.

Minh's team covered it carefully. They explained both sides. They checked every claim. Some claims were unverified; the Gazette said so clearly.

The debate became heated. Some people used **pejorative** words for the villagers, calling them "backward." Minh wrote an editorial against it. "Disagree with the idea," he wrote. "Don't insult people."

The vote was close: 52 percent against joining. The villages stayed independent. "Democracy," said the mayor. "Sometimes it surprises you."

There were other stories that month.

Workers at a local warehouse staged a **walkout** over pay. After two days, the company agreed to raise wages. "We didn't **waver**," said a union leader. "We knew we were right."

A report on **indebtedness** in Riverford showed many families were struggling with debt. Some had taken **subprime** loans, high-risk loans with high interest. "They're trapped," said a charity worker. The council planned a free advice service. The **disbursement** of emergency money began in January.

Grace was given a national award as a **trailblazer** in local journalism. She was the first Black woman to edit a newspaper in the county. At the ceremony, she thanked Minh. "I found him at a careers fair, alone at my table," she said. "Best hire I ever made." Minh cried. Grace pretended not to notice.

Lan talked on the podcast about English as a **lingua** franca: a shared language that people from different countries use to communicate. "English is the world's lingua franca now," she said. "But it's not owned by England. It belongs to everyone who speaks it." She also explained **transitive** verbs, verbs that need an object, for listeners studying grammar, and the joy of **wordplay** in English jokes.

Ffion talked about a **biome**: a large area with similar plants and animals, like a rainforest or a desert. "The **primeval** forests of Europe are almost gone," she said. "We must protect what's left."

Hana talked about eating disorders, including **bulimia**. "It's an illness, not a choice," she said. "People need help, not judgment." Many listeners wrote to thank her for talking about it openly.

A man was arrested for trying to **impersonate** a police officer to get into old people's homes and steal. Inspector Shah warned listeners: "Always check identity. Real police never mind."

Helen Ward talked about history: the **subjugation** of many countries by empires, and the long **disengagement** that followed, as countries became independent. "There's a commonality in all these stories," she said. "People want to rule themselves."

Leo's river trust used computer optimisation to plan where to plant trees for the best effect. "Science helps us use every pound well," he said.

Through all of it, Minh visited Grandma Rose every day. She was home again, after hospital, but very weak. She slept most of the day. When she was awake, her **demeanour** was calm and kind. She was still **gregarious**: she wanted visitors, stories, laughter. "Don't tell me about sad things," she said. "Tell me about the town."

So Minh told her everything. The vote. The walkout. Grace's award. She listened, eyes closed, smiling.

"You know," she said one evening, "when you came, you were a scared boy. Now you're the man who tells this town its own story. You **outdo** all of us."

"Not you, Grandma," said Minh. "Never you."

She laughed softly. "Flattery. Good. I taught you that." Then she was quiet. "I've had a long life, Minh. Ninety-four years. I'm ready. Not today. But soon."

Minh held her hand. He didn't trust his voice.

"Promise me something," she said. "Keep telling stories. Keep this family together. And teach little Rose to make my cake."

"I promise," said Minh.

"Good." She closed her eyes. "Now go home. Your daughter needs you. Reposition my pillow first, though. It's fallen again."

He did. Then he kissed her forehead and went home through the snow, with the **permanence** of her words in his heart. An **insufficiently** strong word, "love," he thought. But it was the only one he had.

He thought about the protesters' tents by the river, as poor as a **shanty**, and the councillors' glossy offices. He thought about **premarital** advice she'd given Hana years ago: "Be patient with him." Hana had been. **Exemplify** kindness, she had always said. She did.
