# Tuan's Band

Minh's best friend Tuan had a new hobby. He wanted to start a band. "We will be famous," he said. "We will play concerts all over the country."

"But you can't sing," Minh said.

"I can learn. And you can play the guitar."

"I can't play the guitar either."

"You can learn too," Tuan said. "It's fun!"

So they started a band with two other boys from school. One boy played the drums, and a girl played the piano. Later, another girl joined with her violin. They were a team now. Their goal was simple: to play one real show. They practiced every Saturday in Tuan's garage. They were very loud. The neighbors were not happy.

Minh was busy with his studies, but he enjoyed the band. It was his free time. He played the guitar badly, and he wrote songs in English. "English songs are cool," Tuan said. "Everybody likes American songs."

In December, the school had a big party for Christmas. There was a show in the school theater. Each class could do something: a dance, a song, a story, a film. Tuan's band decided to play two songs.

Before the show, Tuan was nervous. "What if we lose? What if they don't like us?"

"It's not a race or a football match," Minh said. "Nobody wins or loses. Just have fun."

On the night, the theater was full. There were teachers, parents and students. A boy from another class did a magic show. Two girls did a Korean dance. A group of younger children showed how they draw and paint, and their favorite pictures. Some older students showed a short movie.

Then it was the band's turn. Tuan stood at the front. Minh's hands were shaking. The first song was okay. The second song was terrible. Tuan forgot the words, and Minh played the wrong notes. But then something funny happened. Tuan started to dance. He jumped, he kicked his legs and he threw his hat to the people. A girl in the front tried to catch it. Everybody laughed and stood up and danced too. At the end, they clapped and shouted, "More! More!"

"We won!" Tuan said.

"It's not a competition," Minh said, but he was smiling.

After the show, Ms. Huong talked to them. She was with a young American teacher, Mr. Jake, who was in Vietnam for a year. Mr. Jake laughed and said, "Great show, guys! In America, we have big parties at Christmas too. And in spring, we celebrate **Easter**, with chocolate eggs."

He told them about sports in America: baseball, basketball, volleyball, and football. "But American football is different from your football," he said. "You call it soccer in America."

"In England, they say football," Ms. Huong said.

"Do you like sport?" Minh asked.

"I love it! In winter, I like to **skate** and ski. In summer, I like tennis, running and swimming. I go to the gym every day to keep fit."

Minh liked Mr. Jake. He spoke very fast, but he always explained everything. He talked about American festivals and weddings, and about the page of a magazine where his photo was once. Minh wanted to speak English like him one day.

On the way home, Tuan said, "Tonight was the best night of my life. Better than a gift, better than a present from Santa."

Minh agreed. He did not know it yet, but it was also one of his last Christmases in Vietnam for a long time. He played with a doll for his little sister and flew a kite with her the next week, but his head was already full of other countries and another language.

He decided to ask Mr. Jake one question the next day: "Can you help me practice for a scholarship interview?" He was ready to tell the world what he wanted. He even wrote it down, like a puzzle he wanted to solve.
