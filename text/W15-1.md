# The Stolen Laptop

One Saturday in July, something bad happened at The Blue Cup. A customer, a young woman who was a computer programmer, went to the toilet. She left her laptop on the table, with the charger and a cable still connected to the wall. When she came back, the laptop was gone.

"Somebody stole my laptop!" she shouted. "All my work is on it! My software, my program, everything!"

Daniel ran over. Minh looked around the café. Near the door, a man in a gray hoodie was walking out quickly with a bag. "Him!" Minh said. He ran after the man. But the thief was fast. He jumped on a bike and was gone.

Minh came back without breath. "I'm sorry," he said. "I couldn't catch him."

The police came. A young police officer took notes. "There's a lot of crime like this in summer," she said. "They don't **rob** banks. They steal laptops and phones. Breaking the law is easy when people leave their things on the table."

"Can you find it?" the woman asked.

"Do you have a program to find your laptop? Is it connected to a network?"

The woman checked her phone. She opened an app. "Yes! My laptop is still online! It's not offline yet. I can see it on the map. It's two streets away!"

The police officer called her colleagues on the radio. Ten minutes later, they called back. They found the man in a park, trying to delete everything on the laptop. They brought it back. The woman almost cried. "Thank you!" she said to Minh. "You saw him. You helped."

The next week, the story was in the Riverford Gazette. There was a short article with a big headline: "Café worker helps catch laptop thief." There was even a photo of Minh, looking surprised. Daniel put it on the wall. "You are a **celebrity** now," he said.

Mia and Leo thought it was the funniest thing ever. Mia sent the photo to everyone in her chat group. Leo wanted to upload it to the school website. Tom printed it and put it on the fridge.

Even the local radio wanted to talk to him. A **presenter** called Minh and asked him to come on her programme. "We **broadcast** every afternoon," she said. "Each **episode** is short, ten minutes. Our audience loves local heroes."

Minh was nervous. In the studio, there were microphones, screens and a lot of electronic equipment. Every **gadget** looked expensive. "Don't worry," said the presenter. "Just talk like you're talking to a friend."

"What did you think when you saw the thief?" she asked.

"I didn't think," said Minh. "I just ran. Maybe it's because I watch too many cartoons. In cartoons, the good guy always wins."

The presenter laughed. "And in real life?"

"In real life, he was faster than me. Technology caught him, not me. The hardware and the software did the work."

After the show, Minh went home. Grandma Rose was watching a documentary about the queen. Before that, there was a quiz show and a comedy series she liked. "Did you hear me on the radio?" Minh asked.

"Of course," Grandma said. "Everybody did. I told everyone in the street. Even Mrs. Taylor next door, who only watches the news channel. She says you're a hero. But she also says the king should have more power and the American president is too loud, and the government should make a new rule about everything, so I don't trust her opinion."

Minh laughed. He checked his phone. He had a hundred new messages and a long text from Lan. She had seen the article. "My brother is famous in England!" she wrote. "Are you meeting the prince and princess now?"

"No," he wrote back. "But I got a free cake from Daniel."

"That's better," Lan replied.

That night, Minh changed every password on his phone and his laptop. "Just in case," he said. Then he went to search on the web for a better lock for his bike. He found a good website and decided to download a safety app. He did not want to be the next victim. A friend from the English club sent him a comic about a thief in a coffee shop. He laughed and saved it.

He learned something important: always watch your bag, and always keep a copy of your work. A digital copy is safer than paper. Grandma Rose, of course, still kept her photos on an old DVD and her music on a CD. "If you click a button, it can disappear," she said. "Paper doesn't disappear."

"Paper burns, Grandma," said Minh.

"Then don't play with matches," she said, and went back to her favourite channel, where there was never an advert, to wait for her program.

Before bed, Minh tried to connect to the café's internet to send Daniel a message, but he couldn't. He would receive Daniel's reply in the morning: "The ad for your job worked better than I thought. Now we have a hero. Every café needs one."
