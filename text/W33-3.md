# Goodbye, Riverford

The last month in England was busy. There was so much to do: pack, say goodbye, finish stories. And there was one more surprise. Diana, Minh's agent, called. "A publisher wants your book," she said. "*Two Rivers* will be published next spring. As a real book, and an e-book. Congratulations."

Minh could not speak. When he finally could, he called everyone. His parents. The Parkers. Hana. Mrs. Green at the library, who screamed so loudly that the whole library heard.

The Parkers had a goodbye party for Minh and Hana. The whole town seemed to come. Daniel closed The Blue Cup for the night and made it a private party. Pat made a **sugar-free** cake for Grandma Rose and a normal one for everyone else. Tom grilled steaks, too **well-done** as always.

Margaret came from Scotland, **well-dressed** in a blue suit. Gareth and Megan came from Wales with Ffion. Mr. Okafor came with his whole family, including Grace, who arrived late and said she was "only staying ten minutes." She stayed until midnight.

There were speeches. Daniel spoke first. "When Minh first came to my café, he couldn't make a cappuccino," he said. "Now he can make a cappuccino, save a café, find a lost friend and write a book. I want to declare him an honorary citizen of Riverford."

Everyone cheered.

Then Grace spoke. She was not good at speeches. "I'll be brief," she said. "When Minh came to my table at the careers fair, I assumed he was lost. My first perception was: another student who'll quit in a week. That assumption was wrong. My **instinct** is usually right. This time, my intuition failed. I **confess** I was wrong. He's the best young journalist I've worked with." She paused. "I don't usually praise people. Please don't tell anyone."

Then it was Grandma Rose's turn. She stood up slowly with her stick. The room went quiet.

"I'm an old woman," she said. "I've seen a lot of people come and go. But this boy..." She stopped. Her voice was shaking. "This boy gave me back my best friend. He gave this family a fourth child. He gave this town a voice. And now he's going to Japan with a lovely girl, and I'm too old to go with them." She looked at Minh. "Come back. That's all. Come back, and bring stories."

Minh could not speak. He just hugged her. Everyone was crying, even Leo, who said he had something in his eye.

At the end of the night, Leo told everyone his own news. His football team had won the **quarter-final** and the **semi-final** of the county cup. The final was next week. "You'll miss it," he said to Minh. "But I'll send you a video. Even the warm-up."

Mia showed him a present: a painting of The Blue Cup, with everyone inside. "So you don't forget us," she said.

"I could never forget you," said Minh.

The next week, Minh and Hana went to the airport. They checked their bags. They walked past the **duty-free** shops full of perfume and whisky. They sat at the gate.

Minh looked out at the planes. He thought about the boy who had arrived four years ago with a heavy suitcase and Lan's T-shirt. That boy couldn't buy a bus ticket. He had a lot of **old-fashioned** notions about England from films. He thought everyone drank tea and wore hats.

So much had changed. He had lived in an old house with an English family, then in a **semi-detached** house turned into flats, above Mr. Okafor. He had eaten **fast-food** with Sam at two in the morning and fancy dinners with Grace. He had stayed in a **run-down** hostel in London and a brand-new hotel in Madrid, with an **en-suite** bathroom that felt like a palace. He had bought second-hand clothes, sold-out concert tickets and out-of-stock books. He had visited a **self-service** laundromat every Sunday. He had interviewed a **middle-aged** self-employed builder, a girl from a **single-sex** school and the owner of an **eco-friendly** café, which was really The Blue Cup. He had learned to keep his notes **up-to-date** and to remember every log-in and password.

"What are you thinking?" asked Hana.

"That I've changed," said Minh. "Some people would remark that I'm more English now. I don't know. My viewpoint is different. My reasoning is different. I think in two languages."

"Is that good or bad?"

Minh thought. "Good," he concluded. "Two languages, two homes, two families. Not an all-inclusive holiday. A real life."

Hana took his hand. "I want to clarify one thing," she said. "Japan is not forever. We'll come back. To Riverford, to Vietnam, everywhere. Agreed?"

"Agreed," said Minh. "I'll notify the Parkers."

She laughed. "You sound like a newspaper."

The announcement came: their flight was boarding. Minh stood up. He had no motive except love, and no plan except to see where it led. He didn't want to **insult** the past by being sad. He wanted to emphasize the good. He took one last look at England through the window.

"Goodbye, Riverford," he said quietly. "Thank you."

Then he got on the plane and flew east, toward the sun.
