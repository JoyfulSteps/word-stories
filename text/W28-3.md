# Remembrance

In November, Minh wrote a story about Pete, the homeless man. Grace published it. It was very popular. A local charity read it and found Pete a room in a hostel. Pete came to the Gazette office to say thank you. He was wearing a red paper flower on his coat: a poppy.

"It's for Remembrance Day," he explained. "We remember the soldiers who died in wars."

Pete had been in the **military** for twelve years. First in the army, then in the **navy**. He had served in conflicts in the Middle East. "I saw **violence** you can't imagine," he said quietly. "**Violent** things. Friends died. When I came home, I couldn't sleep. I couldn't work. That's how I ended up on the street."

He told Minh about the war. The enemy, the **allies**, the **troops**. He talked about a **battle** where his **squad** was attacked and three men died. "Every **weapon** you see in films, I've held," he said. "A **gun** is heavy. Not just in your hands."

Minh did not know what to say. He thought of his grandmother's stories about the war in Vietnam. Every country had its wars. Every war had its victims.

For Remembrance Day, the town had a ceremony at the war monument. Minh went with the Parkers. Grandma Rose wore her late husband's medals. "He fought in Korea," she said. "He never talked about it."

At eleven o'clock, everybody was silent for two minutes. Even the birds seemed quiet. Minh thought about all the soldiers who had died: British, Vietnamese, everyone. Old men in their uniforms stood very straight. A young soldier played a sad song on a trumpet.

After the ceremony, there was a small **demonstration** at the edge of the square. A group of young people held signs against war. "No more **invasions**!" they shouted. "Stop the bombs!" Some old people were angry. They thought it was disrespectful. A police officer stood nearby, just in case. But there was no **riot**, only shouting.

Minh talked to both sides. One of the protesters said, "We respect the soldiers. We just don't want new wars. Every country that **invades** another creates new **terrorism**, new **terrorists**, new victims."

An old soldier said, "I understand. I don't like war either. But sometimes you have to fight. My father fought against an **invasion** in 1940."

Minh wrote both stories. Grace was pleased. "Good," she said. "No easy answers. That's life."

The next week, there was happier news. Leo was playing football again. After a year, his knee was strong. His first match back was on Saturday. Minh went to write about it for the sports page.

It was a local league match. The **opponents** were Leo's old **rivals**, a team from the next town. The **squad** was nervous. Leo was more nervous. He had only played **amateur** football, but he was an athletic, natural sportsman. "I don't care if we lose," he said. "I just want to play."

In the first half, Leo was careful. He was afraid of his knee. Then, slowly, he became more confident. He began to **sprint** down the side of the pitch like a real **sprinter**. He learned to **dribble** past players again. He made a strong **tackle**. A player kicked his leg and the referee gave a **foul**. Leo got up. He was fine.

In the second half, the ball came to him in the air. He hit it before it touched the ground, a perfect **volley**. It **bounced** once and went into the goal. Victory! The record score for the season was two-nil. Their defeat of the old rivals was the best news of the year. There was even a penalty at the end, and Leo's friend Jamal scored it.

After the match, Minh interviewed Leo. "How do you feel?"

"Amazing," said Leo. "My **workout** every day for a year was worth it. All those boring exercises." He looked at his knee. "And I've learned I don't need to be a professional athlete. I can play for fun and study to be a vet. I can do both."

Minh wrote the story. The headline was: "Leo Parker: The Comeback." Grace put it on the back page with a big photo. Leo bought ten copies. "One for every wall in the house," he said.

Some things are forbidden in journalism, Grace told Minh later: lying, stealing stories, hurting innocent people. But some things are permitted, even encouraged. Like writing about people you love, with honesty. "Just tell me first next time," she said. "That's my permission rule." Pete came to the match too. He shook Leo's hand. Two survivors, Minh thought. Two kinds of comeback. One religious woman at the match said it was a small miracle. The sportswoman who coached the girls' team said it was hard work. Both were right. When the players began to **shoot** practice balls again, and the last runner finished his final **lap**, Minh turned off his recorder.
