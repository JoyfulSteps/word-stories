# The Final

Leo's football team was in the final of the summer **tournament**. It was the biggest match of the year. If they won, each player got a gold **medal**, and the team got a big cup.

The whole family came to watch. Even Grandma Rose came, with a blanket and a flask of tea. "I've been a supporter of this team for seventy years," she said. "I watched Tom play here when he was a boy. He was terrible."

The **pitch** was green and wet after the rain. Next to it, there was a tennis court and a running track. Some children were playing tennis with old **rackets** and a broken net. The two teams came out. The **referee** blew his whistle. The game started.

Leo's **trainer**, Mr. Hughes, was a tall man with a loud voice. "Run, Leo! Run!" he shouted. Leo ran. He was fast and sporty. After his accident earlier in the year, he had done a lot of training and fitness work. His leg was strong again.

The other team was very good. At half time, Leo's team was losing one-nil. In the changing room, Mr. Hughes talked to the players. "Don't be losers," he said. "I don't care if we lose. I care if we give up. Play for your teammates. Play for each other."

In the second half, Leo's team played better. After sixty minutes, Leo got the ball. He ran past two players and kicked it hard. Goal! The supporters went crazy. Grandma Rose stood up and shouted, "That's my grandson!" She dropped her tea.

Five minutes before the end, the other team scored again. Two-one. Leo's head went down. But in the last minute, his best friend, Jamal, scored with his head. Two-two!

Then there were penalties. Leo was the last player. If he scored, his team would win. If he missed, they would lose. He stood in front of the ball. Behind the goal, a little boy on a bike, in a big blue helmet, held his breath. Everyone was quiet. Mia closed her eyes. Leo ran and kicked.

Goal!

"We beat them!" Leo shouted. "We're the winners!" The whole team jumped on him. The referee laughed. Tom hugged Anna. Minh shouted so loudly that he lost his voice.

That evening, there was a party at the football club. Leo showed everyone his medal. He wore it all night, even in bed. "I'm going to keep it forever," he said. "It's the start of my collection. I'm going to collect a medal every year."

At the party, Minh talked to Mr. Hughes. "What do you do in your **spare** time?" Minh asked. "Football too?"

"No!" Mr. Hughes laughed. "In my **leisure** time, I do **sewing** and **knitting**. My wife taught me. It's very **relaxing**. After shouting at boys all week, I need something quiet."

Minh was surprised. In England, people had a lot of hobbies. Grandma Rose liked gardening, chess and playing cards. Anna liked painting and drawing. Tom liked photography and cooking, especially **baking** bread on Sundays. Mia liked dancing and singing. Leo liked football and, more and more, hiking and walking in the hills.

"What about you?" Mr. Hughes asked. "What are your interests?"

"I don't know," said Minh. "I study and work. I don't have much time for any activity."

"You need an **interest** outside work," said Mr. Hughes. "Something for you. Join a club. Collect something. Life is more **exciting** with a hobby."

The next week, Minh got an invitation from Sam. "Come to my photography club. We walk around town and take pictures." Minh decided to go to the next meeting. Then, maybe, a cooking club. Or chess with Grandma Rose. He would invite her to teach him.

Leo's medal stayed on the kitchen wall for a long time. Every time Minh saw it, he remembered the final, the rain, the tea on the grass and Grandma Rose shouting, "That's my grandson!"
