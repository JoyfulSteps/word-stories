# Spain

In August, while Hana was in Japan, Minh finally went to Spain with Sam. Sam had promised it for two years. "Bring **sunscreen**," he said. "Lots of sunscreen."

They planned a two-week **itinerary**. First, Madrid. Then Sam's family village in the mountains. Then the south coast. They would travel like real **backpackers**: cheap buses, cheap hostels, everything in one bag.

At the airport in Madrid, they went through **customs** and **immigration**. The officer looked at Minh's Vietnamese passport for a long time. "Holiday?" she asked. "Yes," said Minh. "With my friend." She stamped it and smiled. "Welcome to Spain."

They changed some money into the local currency, euros. Sam had bought travel **insurance** for both of them. "Just in case," he said. "My uncle broke his leg in Portugal once. It cost a fortune."

Madrid was hot, loud and beautiful. They **wandered** through the old streets for hours. They visited museums and parks and ate dinner at ten o'clock at night, like Spanish people. Every famous **attraction** was full of **sightseers** with cameras. Some streets were very **touristy**, with expensive restaurants and souvenir shops. "Let's go somewhere real," said Sam. He took Minh to a small bar where only old Spanish men drank coffee. It was perfect.

The hostel in Madrid was **overbooked**. When they arrived, the receptionist said, "I'm sorry, there's no **vacancy**. We have no **vacancies** at all. All our beds are full." It was not the **arrival** they hoped for. Sam argued with her in fast Spanish. In the end, she gave them an upgrade: a private room for the same price. "Spanish is a useful language," said Sam proudly.

After three days, they took a bus to Sam's village. The bus was old and slow. Along the road, they saw a **cyclist** climbing the mountain in the heat and a young man trying to **hitchhike**, holding a sign. A **motorist** stopped and picked him up. "In my village, everyone hitchhikes," said Sam. "Buses come twice a day."

The village was small and **peaceful**, on top of a hill. Sam's grandmother cried when she saw him. She hugged Minh too, as if she had known him forever. She cooked for them every day. She didn't speak English, and Minh didn't speak Spanish, but they understood each other through food and smiles.

From the village, they went on an **excursion** into the mountains. It was a long hike, six hours of walking up and down. They trekked through forests and along rivers. At the top, there was a view of the whole valley. "This is better than any **luxury** hotel," said Minh.

Then they went south, to the sea. The beaches were full of **holidaymakers** from all over Europe: Germans, Dutch, British. Minh forgot to use his sunscreen on the first day. He got a terrible **sunburn**. His back was red for a week. "I told you," said Sam.

On the beach, they met an old **traveler**, a real **globetrotter**. He was seventy and had visited a hundred and twenty countries. He had been on an **expedition** to the North Pole and a long **voyage** across the Pacific on a small boat. "The world is big," he said. "But people are the same everywhere. Remember that."

He gave them a **brochure** for a boat trip to Morocco, just across the sea. "Africa is only fourteen kilometers away," he said. "Very **exotic**. Very different."

They didn't go. They didn't have enough money. "Next time," said Sam.

On the last night, they sat on the beach and watched the sunset. Minh sent a photo to Hana in Japan. It was morning there. She replied: "Beautiful! But where's your sunscreen?"

"Everyone is worried about my sunscreen," said Minh.

"Because you're very red," said Sam.

The next morning, they flew home. Minh's suitcase was full of Spanish cheese, olive oil and a heavy jar of honey from Sam's grandmother. His head was full of new memories. And his back was still a little red.

It was the best holiday of his life. He wanted to **sightsee** more, travel more, see everything. But first, there was a café to save.
