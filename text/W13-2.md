# The Journey to London

In May, Minh and Sam decided to go to London for the weekend. It was Minh's first trip to the **capital**.

"How do we get there?" Minh asked.

"Train is fast but expensive," Sam said. "The coach is slow but cheap. Let's take the coach."

The coach left at seven in the morning from the bus station. There were about forty **passengers**, mostly students and old people. The driver said hello and the coach departed on time. It went through the **suburbs**, past houses and gardens, and then onto the **motorway**. "In America, it's called a **highway**," Minh said. "Tom taught me." Sam rolled his eyes. "Tom teaches you too much American."

On the motorway, there were cars, a big **lorry** full of vegetables, a white **van**, and once, a **helicopter** flying low over the fields. The speed limit was seventy miles an hour. Halfway, they stopped at a service station. The driver put **petrol** in the coach. "Diesel, actually," said Sam. "Big coaches use diesel **fuel**."

Then, near London, there was a problem. The **engine** made a strange noise. The coach slowed down and stopped at the side of the road. The driver got out and looked at the **wheels**. One **tire** was flat. "Sorry, everyone," he said. "We have to wait for help."

They waited for an hour. Some people were angry. An old lady told them about a crash she saw on this route last year. "The **brakes** didn't work," she said. Minh did not want to hear this.

Finally, a new coach came, and they arrived in London at one o'clock. The city was huge, crowded and loud. Minh held his bag close. They went down to the underground. "In London, it's the Underground or the Tube," Sam said. "In New York, it's the subway. In Paris, it's the metro."

On the platform, a train came every two minutes. They found a seat and went through the dark tunnels toward the center. Sam said "towards". "Both are fine," said Minh. "This is the oldest underground train in the world," Sam said. "Over a hundred and fifty years old."

In the center, London was a mix of historic buildings and modern glass towers. On the streets, there were red buses, black taxis, bicycles, an electric **scooter** and a big, loud **motorcycle**. In some parts of the city, there was even a tram.

In the afternoon, they took a **ferry** on the River Thames. They went along the river, past famous buildings. Minh stood at the front of the boat. A **crew** member told them about each building. On the river, there were small boats, a **canoe** with two people in it, and a big white **yacht**. "That belongs to a millionaire," said the captain of the ferry. "Probably."

"Do you like **sailing**?" Minh asked Sam.

"Yes! In Spain, my uncle has a small boat. We **sail** every summer. I want to be a **pilot** of a boat one day. Wait, is that the word?"

"I think a pilot flies a plane," said Minh. "A boat has a captain."

"Then I want to be a captain."

Across the river, opposite the ferry stop, there was a huge wheel, the London Eye. "Tomorrow," said Sam. "Tonight, we find our hostel and eat."

The journey was long and difficult, but Minh was happy. He was in London, the local people were busy and nobody looked at him, and for the first time, he did not feel like a foreigner. He was just another person in a big city.

There was no parking anywhere, Sam noticed. "Good thing we didn't drive," he said.
