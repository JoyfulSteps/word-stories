# The Letter

After the interview, Minh had to wait two months. Those were the longest two months of his life.

At first, he tried to forget about it. He went to school, he played in the band, he helped his father at the shop. But every day, he checked his email. Morning, afternoon, evening. Nothing.

"Can I stop thinking about it?" he asked Grandma. "I want to forget, but I can't."

"You can't forget something you hope for," Grandma said. "Hope is like a cat. It stays in the house, even if you don't call it."

"Shall I make you some tea?" she also asked. "Yes, please," Minh said. Tea was the only thing that helped.

Tuan was not very helpful. "Maybe they lost your email. Maybe they chose another person. Maybe the college is closed." Minh threw a pillow at him. "No," he said. "Too many maybes. Let me hope a little."

Then the exams came. The final exams were very difficult. For a month, Minh did nothing but study. He did not see his friends. He did not play the guitar. The house was quiet, because everybody knew: Minh must study. His mother said, "You should rest a little." His father said, "You must eat." Grandma said, "You might like some soup." Minh said, "I will, later," but he did not move from his desk.

Some questions were easy. Some were hard. In the maths exam, Minh made a mistake and wrote the wrong answer. He knew it when he left the room. "Is it true or false that I failed?" he asked Tuan. "False," Tuan said. "One wrong answer is not the end of the world."

After the exams, Minh was empty. "I am so tired," he said. "I have been a student for twelve years, and being a student is hard work. Does it ever end?" His father laughed. "It never ends. Your father has to learn something new every day too. Keep going." He slept for two days. Then he woke up and checked his email again. Still nothing. His hope was high in the morning and low at night. His mood went up and down like a fast, slow, fast song.

"You need to change something," his mother said. "Go out. See your friends. Stay busy."

So he went to the river with Tuan. They walked around the old market first, to find a quick snack. It was great to be outside. They sat under a tree and talked about everything: girls, football, life. "I'll miss you if you go," Tuan said quietly. "Who will listen to my terrible songs?"

"I'll still listen, on the phone," Minh said. "And maybe I won't go at all."

"Even if they say no," Tuan said, "you can begin again next year. It doesn't mean the end." It was an interesting idea: for once, Tuan was right.

Then, one morning in June, Minh's phone made a sound. He looked at it. An email from the college. His hands were shaking. He could not open it. "Mum!" he called. "Mum, come here!"

His mother came running from the kitchen. Dad came from the shop. Grandma came slowly from her room. Lan came from the bathroom with toothpaste on her face.

Minh opened the email. He read the first line. Then he read it again. He turned the phone so they could see.

"Dear Minh, we are very happy to tell you..."

For a moment, nobody said anything. Then Lan screamed. Mum started to cry. Dad hugged him so hard that he could not breathe. Grandma sat down on a chair and said, "I knew it. I knew it."

"Full scholarship," Minh said. "They pay for everything. Classes, books, everything. I start in September. I arrive in England at the end of August."

That night, the whole street knew. Neighbors came with food and drinks. Ms. Huong came and hugged him. "I told you," she said. "When, not if." Even Mr. Ba, now very old, came with his stick. "Your grandfather would be proud," he said.

Minh tried to remember everything about that night: every face, every word, every sound. He understood something important. He was leaving, but this place would always be his. He hoped he would never forget it. He was sure he would not.

Late at night, the house was full of empty glasses. Minh was alone on the roof, looking at the river. He could hear the water in the dark. "I'm going," he said to it, very softly. "But I'll come back. I promise."

And somewhere in the dark, a fish jumped, as if to say: we will see.
