# The Job Interview

At the end of June, Grandma Rose's nephew Daniel put an advertisement in the window of his café, The Blue Cup: "Part-time staff wanted. Weekends. Apply inside."

"Daniel doesn't like to advertise online," Grandma Rose said. "He likes a sign in the window. You should apply." "Daniel needs help, and you need money."

Minh was nervous. He had never had a real job interview. He filled in the application form carefully and wrote his signature at the bottom. He printed his CV on Tom's old printer, but the printer was broken, so he went to the college library and used the photocopier there. He made one photocopy of his student card too. He put everything in a clean folder.

Sam helped him plan. Sam was also looking for a job. He wanted to be a designer, and he applied to a design firm in the city. "Look," he said, and showed Minh a big file of his work on his laptop. "The company is small, but they have big clients. If they hire me, I'll work with an architect on new buildings."

"What do I say in an interview?" asked Minh.

"Tell them why you want the job. Tell them what you can do. Smile. And never say anything bad about your last boss."

"I've never had a boss."

"Then that's easy."

On Saturday morning, Minh went to The Blue Cup. Daniel was a big man with a beard and a friendly face. He was a musician too. He played guitar on Friday nights in the café. Before he opened the café, he worked for ten years as an accountant at a bank. "I was a banker who hated numbers," he laughed. "Then I decided to retire from that life and make coffee."

The interview was in the small office behind the café. On the desk, there was an old computer with a big screen, a keyboard, a **stapler**, a pile of documents and, strangely, a fax machine. "My father's," said Daniel. "Nobody sends a fax anymore. But I can't throw it away."

Daniel asked his questions. "Why do you want to work here?"

"Because I like people, and I like coffee," said Minh. "And because I need money. My parents are not rich. I want to help them."

"Honest. I like that. Do you have experience?"

"I helped my father at his shop. I can carry heavy things, I can clean, and I learn fast."

Daniel looked at his form. "You speak Vietnamese and English. Good. We have more and more customers from other countries. What about a little Spanish?"

"My friend Sam is teaching me," said Minh. "Hola. That's all."

Daniel laughed. He told Minh about the business. "We don't sell an expensive product. We sell good coffee, good cake and good service. Most of my customers are regulars. There's an old lawyer who comes every morning, a retired judge who does the crossword, a journalist and a young reporter from the Riverford Gazette, a photographer, a computer programmer who works from her smartphone all day, and a lot of students with headphones or earphones in, studying. Some colleagues from the hospital come at night."

"And a model," said a young woman from the counter. "Don't forget the model." She was Daniel's other employee, Jess. "He comes on Sundays. Everybody looks at him."

"And sometimes a **businesswoman** from London who sends angry messages to her team all day," said Daniel. "Being unemployed is better than her job, I think."

At the end, Daniel stood up. "Can you start next Saturday?"

Minh could not believe it. "Yes! Of course!"

"Good. You'll need a lot of energy. Saturday morning is busy. And there's live music on Fridays. Sometimes I need help with the speaker and the microphone. And please don't touch the monitor on the counter. It's for the security camera. We had a guard once, but he ate too much cake."

That evening, Minh sent a message to his family: "I got a job!" His mother replied with a hundred hearts. Sam called an hour later. "I got the job too! A real design firm!" Minh wrote a short report of the day in his diary.

They went to The Blue Cup to celebrate. Daniel gave them free coffee. "Don't get used to it," he said. "From next week, you pay."
