# A Parcel from Home

One morning, the doorbell rang. It was the postman with a big **parcel**. "It's for you, Minh," Anna said. "From Vietnam!"

"In America, we say package," said Tom.

Minh opened it on the kitchen table. His mother sent him many things. There was a packet of green tea, a plastic bag of dried mango, and a metal box of Vietnamese coffee. There was also a little wooden box. Minh opened it. It was made of dark wood, and inside there was a photo of his family.

At the bottom, there was an **envelope** with a Vietnamese **stamp** on it. Inside, his mother wrote: "Eat well. Keep warm. Write in your **diary** every day, like your father did when he was a student." There was also a small **calendar** with photos of Hanoi, and a new **notebook** for his diary.

Minh's mother also sent things for the family. For Grandma Rose, a basket with Vietnamese sweets. For Anna, a bottle of **perfume**. For Mia, a **lipstick** and some **makeup**. For Leo, a football shirt. For Tom, a big container of fish sauce. Tom smelled it and his face changed. "Wow," he said. "That's... strong."

Mia was very happy with her gift. She ran to the bathroom to try the lipstick. When she came back, Grandma Rose looked at her and said, "Very pretty. But you're fifteen. A little less, please."

Then Minh found a small bag at the bottom of the box. Inside, there was **shampoo** and **conditioner**, a **razor**, a **hairbrush**, and a **deodorant**. Minh laughed. "My mother thinks England has no shops!"

"Mothers are the same everywhere," Anna said.

In the afternoon, it was cleaning day. Every Saturday, everybody helped to tidy the house. Grandma Rose gave everyone a job, like a teacher.

Minh had to **sweep** the kitchen floor. Then he had to **wipe** the table and **dust** the shelves in the living room. Leo had to **scrub** the bathroom. "This is the worst job," he said. Mia had to wash the dishes, **rinse** them in clean water and put them away. Tom had to **iron** the shirts. He was very slow. Anna said, "Tom, you **iron** like a man who is afraid of the shirts."

Grandma Rose did the most important job. She sat in her armchair and used a soft cloth to **polish** the old silver spoons. "This is a job for an expert," she said with a smile.

At the end, they had to **hang** the wet clothes in the garden. Mia's **hairdryer** was on the table, and she wanted to dry her clothes with it. "No," said Anna. "The sun is free."

That evening, Minh sat in his room and opened the new notebook. He began to write in his diary. "Today a piece of Vietnam came to England," he wrote. "Everybody liked it, except Tom and the fish sauce."
