# The Newsroom

On Monday at eight o'clock, Minh arrived at the offices of the Riverford Gazette. It was a small, old building on the main street, above a shoe shop. The newsroom had ten desks, five computers and a lot of coffee cups.

Grace introduced him to the team. There was Raj, the deputy editor and Minh's **supervisor**. There was Ellie, a young reporter who had joined as a **trainee** two years ago. There was an old photographer called Bill, who had worked there for forty years. There were two other reporters and a woman who did the marketing and the sales of advertisements.

"This is our whole **workforce**," said Grace. "Ten years ago, we had forty people. Now, it's us. We all do everything."

Raj explained Minh's **duties**. "You'll start with small stories. Local events, school news, council meetings. You'll check facts. You'll answer the phone. It's not glamorous. But every journalist starts here. I was an **apprentice** at a newspaper when I was sixteen. I made coffee for a year."

The workload was heavy. On his first day, Minh wrote three short stories: a cat rescued from a tree, a new bakery opening, and a council decision about parking. He worked from eight until seven. There was no overtime pay. "Interns don't get overtime," said Ellie. "Or a real **paycheck**. You get a little money and a lot of experience."

"What are the **perks** of the job?" Minh joked.

"Free coffee," said Ellie. "Bad coffee, but free."

In the afternoon, Grace sent Minh with Ellie to cover a story. Workers at a local factory were on **strike**. The factory made car parts. The workers' **union** said the company paid them too little. The company said it could not pay more. "Sales are down," said the factory **executive**, a man in an expensive suit. "Export to Europe is down. Demand is down. The **turnover** of the company has fallen by twenty percent."

Outside the factory, the workers stood with signs. One of them was a skilled laborer who had worked there for thirty years. "We work hard," he said. "Manual work, heavy work. Our **earnings** haven't gone up for five years. The **chairman** of the company got a big bonus last year. We got nothing."

Minh listened. He took notes. He asked careful questions. Ellie watched him. "You're good at listening," she said later. "That's the most important skill. Most people only want to talk."

Back at the office, they wrote the story together. Grace read it. She made a lot of changes. "Never take sides," she said. "Tell both stories. The workers' story and the company's story. Let the readers decide."

Minh learned a lot that week. He learned about local industry and commerce, the industrial past of the town and its commercial future. Every small enterprise had a story. He learned how a small organization like the Gazette worked: the administration, the management, the money. He learned that trade between countries could change a small town's life. When supply was low and demand was high, prices went up. When a big corporation closed a factory, a hundred families had no work.

He also met interesting people. A **consultant** who helped small businesses. A young **entrepreneur** who started a company selling Vietnamese-style coffee. "I'm the **founder**," she said proudly. "And the only worker." An expert on local history who had written five books about Riverford.

On Friday, Grace called him into her office. "Well?" she said. "What do you think?"

"It's hard," said Minh. "Very hard. But I love it."

Grace smiled for the first time all week. "Good answer. You've done well for an intern. This internship is three months. If you're still alive at the end, we'll talk."

"Is that a target?" asked Minh.

"That's my objective," said Grace. "You have a lot of responsibility here. A lot of tasks. If you achieve them, that's an achievement. If not, you'll be a very well-trained coffee maker."

On the bus home, Minh was exhausted. But he was smiling. The workplace was small, old and noisy. The pay was terrible. But he had found something he loved. Maybe, one day, he would be unskilled at nothing and skilled at this. He wanted to import every lesson and export every story.
