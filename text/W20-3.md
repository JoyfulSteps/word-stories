# Leo's Long Road

Leo needed **surgery** on his knee. The **surgeon**, a calm man with gray hair, explained everything. "We'll repair the ligament. The operation takes two hours. After that, he'll need months of **therapy**. He can't play football for at least nine months. Maybe a year."

Leo did not say anything. He just stared at the wall.

The operation went well. When Leo woke up, he was **dizzy** and sick. He **vomited** twice. His knee was **swollen** and **painful**. The nurse gave him a **painkiller** and an **injection** against infection. "You'll feel better tomorrow," she said. "You'll have a small **scar**, but that's all." The next day, Leo had a red **rash** on his arm from the tape, but it went away quickly.

He stayed in the hospital **ward** for three days. Everyone came to visit. Grandma Rose brought cake. Mia brought drawings. Minh brought comic books. Hana came during her breaks, in her nurse's uniform. "You're my favourite patient," she told Leo. He smiled for the first time.

When he went home, he had to use **crutches**. For the first week, he could not even climb the stairs. Tom carried him up like a little boy. At the hospital, Leo had seen people in a **wheelchair** and on a **stretcher**, and he knew he was lucky. But he did not feel lucky.

Every week, he went to see a **specialist**, a **therapist** named Sarah. She gave him exercises. They were boring and they hurt. "The knee needs to **heal** slowly," she said. "If you push too hard, you can hurt it again. There's no quick **cure**."

At home, Leo became quiet. He stopped going out. He stopped seeing his friends. He spent all day in his room playing video games. He did not want to talk to anyone. He was depressed.

Anna was worried. "He's not himself," she said. "He's not eating. He's not sleeping. I think the stress is too much for him."

Tom tried to talk to him. Leo shouted at him and closed the door. Mia tried. Leo said, "Leave me alone." Minh decided to try too.

He knocked on Leo's door. "Can I come in?"

"Whatever," said Leo.

Minh sat on the end of the bed. Leo did not look at him. For a long time, neither of them said anything.

"I'm sorry about your knee," said Minh finally.

"It's not just my knee," said Leo. "It's everything. Football was my life. I was going to play for a big team one day. Now I'm **disabled**. I'm useless."

"You're not disabled," said Minh. "You're injured. There's a difference. A **disability** is forever. This is for a year."

"A year is forever," said Leo.

Minh thought. "When I came to England," he said, "I felt like a **deaf** person. I couldn't understand anyone. I felt stupid. I felt stressed every day. Sometimes I cried at night. I wanted to go home."

Leo looked at him for the first time. "Really? You?"

"Really. Depression is not only for old people or sick people. It can happen to anyone. But it gets better. Slowly. Like a knee."

Leo was quiet. Then he said, "What helped you?"

"People," said Minh. "Your family. Grandma Rose. Sam. And doing small things. One step at a time. Like your exercises. They're boring, but they work."

The next day, Leo did his exercises without complaining. The day after, he went downstairs for dinner. The week after, he went to watch his team play. He sat on the side of the pitch on his crutches. His teammates cheered when they saw him.

"Sarah says I'm doing well," he told Minh a month later. "She says my knee is getting stronger. And my **prescription** for painkillers is finished. I don't need them anymore."

"That's great," said Minh.

"Doctors can examine you and give you a **vaccine** to prevent some diseases," said Leo, "but they can't stop you from being stupid. I was stupid to give up."

"You weren't stupid," said Minh. "You were sad. Different thing."

Leo smiled. "Thanks, Minh. Really."

"Anytime," said Minh. "And Leo?"

"Yeah?"

"Your room smells terrible. Open a window. You could **infect** the whole house."

Leo threw a pillow at him. Minh laughed. It was the old Leo again. Almost.
