# This Week in Riverford: New Life

This month's episode was different. It was recorded in the hospital.

On a rainy night in March, Hana gave birth to a baby girl. She was small, healthy and very loud. They named her Rose Mai: Rose for Grandma Rose, Mai for Minh's grandmother. Minh held her for the first time at three in the morning. He couldn't stop crying.

Grandma Rose, now ninety, came to the hospital the next day. She held the baby carefully. "Rose," she said. "Little Rose." Then she looked at Minh. "I'm honoured. Truly. Now I can die happy."

"Not yet, Grandma," said Minh. "She needs to know you."

"Well," said Grandma Rose, "I'll try to stay a bit longer then."

Minh recorded a short episode in the hospital room, with Hana's permission. Hana was tired but happy. Tom, Anna, Mia and Leo came. Minh's parents and Lan joined by video from Vietnam. Hana's parents joined from Japan. Three countries, one baby.

They talked about **child-rearing** in different cultures. Hana's mother said Japanese parents were strict but **laid-back** about some things. Minh's mother said Vietnamese grandmothers would do most of the work. "In England," said Anna, "nobody knows what they're doing. We just try."

They talked about languages. Rose Mai would grow up with three: English, Vietnamese and Japanese. "She'll be **code-switching** before she can walk," said Lan. "Changing languages in the middle of a sentence, like all bilingual children."

Mia asked what kind of person they hoped Rose would be. Hana thought. "**Open-minded**," she said. "Kind. **Well-rounded**: good at many things, not just one."

"**Self-disciplined**," said Minh's father. "Like her father."

"Happy," said Grandma Rose. "With good **self-esteem**. That's all."

The episode was short, but listeners loved it. Hundreds of messages came. Some people sent baby clothes. Daniel sent a cake. The youth council sent a card signed by forty teenagers.

The rest of the month's news continued, but Minh let Ellie from the Gazette host it. She covered big changes in the town. Some were good, some worrying.

The old factory area was being redeveloped. There would be a **high-rise** building, twelve floors, the tallest in Riverford. Some people thought it was ugly. Others thought it was **ground-breaking**: a **carbon-neutral** building with solar panels, gardens on every floor and its own water system. Its design was based on **peer-reviewed** research.

The town also planned new transport: **ride-sharing** apps for the villages and a **car-pooling** program for workers. A charity collected **e-waste**, old phones and computers, to recycle them. "Old electronics contain **non-renewable** metals," said the organiser. "We must recover them."

A group of young people started an **e-sports** club at the youth centre: competitive video games. Some parents worried. Others were **tech-savvy** and supportive. "It's a real sport now," said James Morgan, the young councillor. "There are professional players. It teaches teamwork."

**Eco-tourism** was growing in the region too: visitors who came for nature, walking and wildlife, not just shopping. The council promoted **health-conscious** activities: walking routes, cycling paths and a farmers' market with **nutrient-rich**, local food. A user-friendly app showed all the routes.

At the dig site, Sophie's team had **unearthed** a small gold coin, the best find of the year. Helen Ward told a history story about an **uprising** of farm workers in the 1830s, and a time of **upheaval** in England when industrialisation changed everything. "Old ways of life began to **wane**," she said. "**Westernisation** later spread these changes around the world. **Urbanisation** pulled people into cities."

Grace reported on a **whistleblower** at a national company: an employee who revealed that the company had hidden pollution data. "Whistleblowers are brave," said Grace. "They risk everything for the truth."

There was also inner-city news from the nearby city: a new program for poor neighbourhoods. And a viewership report: the Gazette's online videos had doubled their audience.

Minh listened to the episode at home, holding Rose Mai. She was asleep on his chest. Hana was asleep on the sofa. The rain was falling outside. He thought about his own journey: from a small town by a river in Vietnam, to a small town by a river in England. Now there was a new person in the world, at the start of her own journey.

"Welcome, little one," he whispered. "There's so much to see. You can upskill later. For now, just sleep."
