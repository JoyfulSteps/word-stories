# A Normal Day

By December, Minh knew the Parkers' routine very well. Every day in the house was busy, but every day was a little bit the same.

Tom got up first, at six. He went to the bathroom to **shave**. He always sang while he shaved, very badly. Then Anna got up. She made coffee and set the table for breakfast. Grandma Rose came down at seven, slowly, and sat in her chair by the window.

Then the **rush** began. Leo couldn't find his shoes. Mia couldn't find her phone. Tom couldn't find his keys. "**Hurry**!" Anna shouted. "The bus leaves in five minutes!" Every morning, the same. Every morning, they made it, just.

After everyone left, the house was quiet. Minh's classes started at ten, so he had time to help Grandma Rose. She gave him a **chore** every day. On Monday, he did the **laundry**: he put the dirty clothes in the machine, then hung the **washing** in the garden. On Tuesday, he had to **fold** the clean clothes and lay them on each bed. On Wednesday, he helped with the housework: cleaning the kitchen and the bathroom. On Thursday, he went shopping with her.

"Why do you give me so many jobs?" Minh asked once, laughing.

"Because a busy young man is a happy young man," said Grandma Rose. "And because my back hurts."

In the afternoon, after lunch, Grandma Rose always took a **nap** in her chair. Exactly forty minutes. Then she woke up and said, "I wasn't sleeping. I was thinking."

Minh liked the quiet afternoons. He studied at the kitchen table. Sometimes he helped Grandma Rose feed the birds in the garden or fill the bird bath with water. Sometimes he just sat with a cup of tea and tried to **relax**.

In the evening, everyone came home tired. Dinner was at six. Later, at nine, Grandma Rose liked a small **supper**: tea and a piece of toast. "In my day, supper was the last meal," she said. "Now people eat dinner at nine. Very strange."

Before bed, the children had to **bathe** or shower. Leo always took twenty minutes. Mia always complained.

On the Thursday before Christmas, Minh went shopping with Grandma Rose for the Christmas food. It was a big job. They had a long list.

First, they went to the butcher. Grandma Rose ordered a big **turkey** for Christmas Day. "The biggest you have," she said. "This family eats a lot." She also bought a **ham** for Boxing Day, the day after Christmas, some **bacon** and **sausages** for breakfast, a piece of **lamb** for the weekend and two **steaks** for Tom, "because he'll complain if he doesn't get steak."

Then they went to the fish shop. Minh was excited. He loved **seafood**. There were big pink **prawns**. "In America, they say **shrimp**," said Grandma Rose, before Minh could. He laughed. There was a big **crab** and, in a tank, a live **lobster**, very expensive. "No lobster," said Grandma Rose. "Not even at Christmas."

At home, they had to **unpack** everything. The fridge was full. The freezer was full. Some things had to go in the garage, where it was cold.

That evening, the children got home and saw all the food. "Is it Christmas already?" asked Leo.

"Not yet," said Grandma Rose. "First, we **pack** the presents. Then we decorate the tree. Then Christmas."

They spent the evening wrapping presents and listening to Christmas music. Minh wrapped a present for each person in the family. They were small things: a scarf for Grandma Rose, a book for Anna, a football magazine for Leo, paints for Mia, a funny tie for Tom.

When he went to bed, Minh thought about home. In Vietnam, there was no Christmas, not really. But there was Tet, the New Year, in February. His family would be busy preparing too. Different food, different customs. The same feeling: family, together.
