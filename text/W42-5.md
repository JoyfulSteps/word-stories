# The Decision

The day after the wedding, Minh and Hana sat in Margaret's conservatory with cups of tea. Outside, it was raining. The river was loud.

"So," said Hana. "What's next? You said you had a plan."

Minh had been thinking about it for months. He had to **comprehend** his own feelings first. Now he was ready.

"I want to come back to England," he said. "To Riverford."

Hana didn't look surprised. "Why?"

"I love Japan," said Minh. "And Open Tokyo will be fine without me now. But I keep thinking about Riverford. Grace is getting older. The Gazette needs new ideas. And I have an idea."

"Go on."

"A podcast," said Minh. "Called *Riverford Talks*. Every week, I'd interview someone. People from the town, experts, ordinary people. About big questions: education, the environment, crime, technology, health. Like the IELTS topics we studied years ago." He laughed. "But real. Real people, real conversations."

"You'd be the host?"

"I'd be the host. And Grace would help. And the Gazette would publish the best conversations as articles."

Hana thought about it. "What about my job?"

"There are hospitals in England. Kate says Riverford Hospital needs nurses. You could even work with Dr. Adeyemi again."

Hana was quiet for a long time. Minh waited. He didn't want to **presume** anything. This had to be her decision too.

"I'll miss Japan," she said finally. "My parents. My friends. The food." She paused. "But I've missed England too. The rain. The Parkers. Even the bad coffee." She smiled. "And I want our children to know Grandma Rose."

Minh almost dropped his tea. "Our children?"

"Not yet," said Hana, laughing. "But one day. **Cognitive** development is better in children who grow up with grandparents. I read it in a study."

"Is that true?"

"Probably," said Hana. "It's not **irrational**, anyway."

They called Grace that evening. Minh explained his idea. It was a little **verbose**; he was nervous. Grace listened without interrupting. Then she said, "Yes."

"Yes?"

"Yes. I've been thinking about something similar. Podcasts are **cutting-edge**. Every newspaper wants one. And you have the voice for it. You have **empathy**, **tact** and a good sense of **nuance**. People like talking to you. You build **rapport** quickly."

"Thank you," said Minh.

"Don't thank me yet," said Grace. "You'll need to be **self-confident** but not **self-centered**. You'll interview people who disagree with each other. You'll need to **mediate** like a **mediator**, let both sides talk, and never take sides. When someone says something false, you'll need to **refute** it politely. When they **rebut** you, you'll need to listen. Sometimes you'll need to **concede** a point. You'll need to **reiterate** questions people try to avoid. And when someone **alludes** to something or **insinuates** something without saying it, you'll need to ask them to say it clearly."

"That's a lot," said Minh.

"That's journalism," said Grace. "Welcome back."

After the call, Minh and Hana went for a walk by the river. The rain had stopped. Everything smelled fresh and green.

"Are you scared?" asked Hana.

"A little," said Minh. "It's a new thing. **State-of-the-art** equipment, a new kind of storytelling. I've never done it."

"You've never done anything before you did it," said Hana. "That's how everything starts."

Minh laughed. "That's very wise."

"Grandma Rose said it," said Hana. "At the wedding. I'm just repeating."

Over the next month, they planned everything. Minh talked to Sam about designing the podcast. Sam had a whole studio now: "Very **avant-garde**," he said. They talked about **e-commerce** for merchandise, **e-learning** programs for students, a **fuel-efficient** electric van to record outside. Sam suggested that the first episode be about the history of Riverford, from the **Anglo-Saxon** village to today.

"No," said Minh. "The first episode is about something more important."

"What?"

"Rivers," said Minh. "Why towns are built by rivers. Why people meet by rivers. And why they come back."

Sam laughed. "Of course it is."

In June, Minh and Hana moved back to England. Their **comprehension** of the word "home" had changed completely. Home was not one place. It was many places, connected by people. And for now, it was a small town by a river, with an old castle on a hill, where a family was waiting for them at the station.
