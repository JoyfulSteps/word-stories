# Leo's New Passion

Leo's knee was getting better, but he still could not play football. He was bored and restless. Then, one Saturday in April, his therapist suggested something. "There's a wildlife rescue center outside town. They need volunteers. Maybe you'd like it?"

Leo went, without much hope. He came back at six o'clock, muddy and excited. "It was amazing," he said at dinner. "Did you know there's a **hedgehog** hospital there? They have forty hedgehogs!"

From that day, Leo went every weekend. Minh came with him once, to see what it was like.

The center was in an old farm. It took in wild animals that were hurt or lost. A woman called Jo ran it. She had worked with animals all her life. "We put them back into their natural **habitat** when they're well," she said. "That's the goal."

Leo showed Minh around. There was an **otter** with a hurt leg, swimming in a small pool. There was a family of baby **moles**, tiny and blind, found by a farmer. There was a fox cub whose mother was killed by a car. There was a **seagull** with a broken wing, and a little **sparrow** that a cat had attacked.

"Cats are terrible **predators**," said Jo. "They kill millions of birds every year. The birds are their **prey**."

Leo was good with the animals. He was patient and gentle. He fed the baby hedgehogs with a tiny bottle. "They sleep in a **burrow** in the wild," he said. "Or under a hedge. We make them little houses from boxes."

Minh was surprised. He had never seen Leo like this: calm, careful, interested.

In the office, Jo had pictures of animals from all over the world on the wall. "Before this, I worked in Africa," she said. She showed them photos. A **cheetah**, the fastest animal on land. A **leopard** in a tree. A big gray **rhinoceros**. "We call it a **rhino** for short," she said. "There are very few left. **Hunters** kill them for their horns."

"That's terrible," said Leo.

"It is," said Jo. There was also a photo of a huge **hippopotamus** in a river. "The most dangerous animal in Africa," said Jo. "More dangerous than a lion." And a herd of African **buffalo**. "In Vietnam, you have water **buffalo**, don't you?" she asked Minh.

"Yes, on the farms. They pull the plough in the rice fields. My grandfather had one."

She had also worked in America. She showed a picture of a **jaguar** from the South American jungle, an American **bison** on the prairie, and a huge **moose** in Canada. "A moose can be two meters tall," she said. There were pictures of smaller animals too: a **beaver** building a dam, a **raccoon** with its black mask, and a black and white **skunk**. "Never make a skunk angry," said Jo. "It has a terrible smell."

"What about birds?" asked Leo.

Jo showed him a **peacock** with its beautiful tail, an **ostrich** that could run faster than a horse, and a group of pink **flamingos** standing on one leg. "Flamingos are pink because of the food they eat," she said.

On the way home, Leo talked all the time. "I want to be a vet," he said. "Or work in a place like that. Or in Africa, saving rhinos."

"I thought you wanted to be a football player," said Minh.

Leo was quiet. "Maybe I can do both," he said. "But this feels important. Football is fun. This is... I don't know. It means something."

At home, Mia was in her room with a pet **hamster** her friend had given her. It lived in a cage on her desk and ran in its wheel all night. "Leo, look! I have an animal too," she said.

Leo looked at the hamster for a long time. "His cage is too small," he said seriously. "He needs a bigger **den**. And more things to climb. I'll make him one."

Mia stared at him. "Who are you, and what have you done with my brother?"
