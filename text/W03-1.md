# Grandma's Kitchen

Grandma's kitchen was the heart of the house. Every day, she cooked breakfast, lunch and dinner for the whole family. Minh loved to sit on a small chair next to her and watch.

In the morning, she made rice soup or noodles. Sometimes Dad wanted an egg and some bread with milk. "Strange food," Grandma said, but she made it for him anyway.

For lunch and dinner, there was always rice, and then meat or fish, and vegetables. Grandma bought everything at the market early in the morning: chicken, pork, beef, fresh fish from the river, potatoes, carrots, onions, beans, peas, corn and tomatoes. She did not like the supermarket. "Supermarket vegetables have no taste," she said.

Minh's job was to help. He washed the vegetables and cut the beans. Once, he cut his finger with the knife and cried. Grandma put his finger in cold water and gave him a piece of candy. "A good cook always cuts a finger once," she said. "Now you are a real cook."

Grandma also loved fruit. On the kitchen table, there was always a big bowl of fruit: bananas, oranges, a lemon or two, a pear, an apple, a peach, cherries in summer, and grapes. In the hot season, there was mango, pineapple and watermelon. Minh's favorite was mango. Lan's favorite was strawberry, but strawberries were expensive, so she only had them on her birthday.

When Minh was twelve, his cousin Hoa visited from the city. She was different now. She talked about Western food: pizza, pasta, hamburgers, burgers with cheese, sandwiches, salad. "In the city, we eat pizza every Friday," she said.

Grandma did not know what pizza was. So one weekend, Minh and Hoa decided to make a pizza for the family. They had no oven, so they cooked it in a big pan. They put tomato, cheese and chicken on top. It was not very good. The bottom was black and the cheese was strange. But Grandma ate a big piece and said, "Delicious. Now I know pizza. Tomorrow I will cook real food again."

Everybody laughed.

On special days, the family ate at a restaurant or a small cafe by the river. Minh liked the cafe best. He drank orange juice or lemonade, and Dad drank coffee or tea. Lan wanted cola or soda, but Mum said no. "Water is better," she said. "Too much sugar is bad for your teeth."

For a snack after school, Minh liked a sweet **biscuit** or a cookie with ice in his drink. Grandma did not like sweets very much. "In my time, we had no chocolate, no cake, no candy," she said. "We had fruit and salt and rice, and we were happy."

"Were you really happy?" Minh asked.

Grandma thought for a moment. "Yes," she said. "But I also wanted chocolate."

Years later, in England, Minh ate many meals. He ate fish and chips, roast beef, English breakfast and Tom's terrible pizza. But no meal was as good as a bowl of Grandma's soup, eaten next to her in that small, hot kitchen.
