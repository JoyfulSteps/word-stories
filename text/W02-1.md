# The Old House

Minh's house was old and small, but he loved it. His cousin Hoa lived in an apartment in the city, but Minh preferred a real house. It was a narrow house in the middle of the town, between a shop and a cafe. Downstairs there was a kitchen, a bathroom with a toilet and a shower but no bath, and a front room with a sofa, a table and a television. Everybody called it the TV. Upstairs there were two bedrooms. Minh shared a room with his sister Lan, and Grandma slept in the small room next to it.

There was no garage, so Dad kept his motorbike inside the house at night. There was no real garden either, only a small yard with a gate and a few plants. On the flat roof, Mum dried the clothes in the sun: shirts, trousers, a dress, a skirt, socks, and Dad's work jeans.

In the bedroom, there were two beds, a desk with a lamp, a small shelf for books, a cupboard for clothes and one chair. On the wall, there was a picture of a mountain and a clock that was always ten minutes late. There was one window. From the window, Minh could see the street and the river.

Every morning, Minh put on his school clothes: a white shirt, dark pants and black shoes. In summer, he wore shorts. In winter, it was sometimes cold in the north of Vietnam, so he wore a coat, a hat and a **sweater** that Grandma made for him. The sweater was red and a little too big. "You will grow," Grandma said. He wore it for five years.

Dad wore a cap and old boots for work. Mum wore glasses when she read, and a watch that was a present from Dad. She always carried a big bag with her students' books inside.

One rainy night, when Minh was ten, the river came up. The water came under the door and onto the floor of the house. "Everybody upstairs!" Dad shouted. Outside, the street was a river now. They carried everything they could: the TV, the computer, the radio, the phone, a box of photos, and the chairs. Minh carried his school bag and his sister's shoes. The water was cold and dirty, and it covered the bottom of the stairs.

They sat on the beds upstairs and listened to the rain. There was no light, because the electricity was off. Lan was scared. Minh put his jacket around her. "It's okay," he said. "The house is strong. It's old, but it's strong."

In the morning, the water went down. The kitchen was a mess. The table was wet, the cupboard door was broken, and Mum's mirror in the bathroom had mud on it. The shower did not work for a week. But nobody was hurt.

Everybody on the street helped to clean. People washed the walls and the floors, and they dried the books in the sun. In the evening, Mum made tea for all the neighbors, and everyone drank from her best cup and from old glasses and bowls.

"Why do we live next to the river?" Minh asked his father.

"Because the river is our home," Dad said. "It gives us water, fish and a beautiful view. Sometimes it is angry. But it is still home."

Years later, when Minh saw the river in England from Sam's window, he thought about his old house and that long night upstairs.
