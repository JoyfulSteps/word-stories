# Bad News from Home

One morning in May, Minh got a phone call from Vietnam. It was his mother. She never called in the morning. He knew immediately that something was wrong.

"It's Grandma," his mother said. Her voice was shaking. "She fell in the kitchen last night. She's in the hospital. The doctors think it's her heart."

Minh felt **terrified**. His hands were cold. "Is she... is she going to be OK?"

"We don't know yet. They're doing tests."

After the call, Minh sat on his bed for a long time. He felt **hopeless**. He was eleven thousand kilometers away. He could do nothing. He could not hold her hand. He could not even see her.

He felt **guilt**, too. He had not called her for two weeks. He was busy with work, with college, with Hana. He had been happy, and now he felt bad about it. He felt a deep **regret**.

Grandma Rose found him in his room. She sat next to him without a word. She held his hand. Her **sympathy** helped more than words.

"I want to go home," Minh said. "But the ticket is so expensive. And I have exams in two weeks."

"Wait for news first," said Grandma Rose. "Don't **panic**. Panic never helps."

That day was **miserable**. Minh could not eat, could not study, could not think. He was **homesick** in a way he had never been before. Everything in England felt far away and unreal. Even the rain was **annoying**. Everything **annoyed** him: the noise of the traffic, the TV, Leo's music.

In the evening, Hana came. She brought soup. "Eat," she said. "You need energy. Your grandmother would want you to eat."

"How do you know?"

"Because all grandmothers want everyone to eat. It's the same in Japan."

Minh smiled for the first time all day.

At midnight, his phone rang again. It was Lan this time. Her voice was different. "Minh! Grandma is OK! It's not her heart. It was low blood sugar, and she hit her head when she fell. She has to stay in the hospital for a few days, but she's OK! She's asking for you!"

Minh felt a huge **relief**. He was so **relieved** that he started to laugh and cry at the same time. Hana held him. Downstairs, Grandma Rose heard him and came up the stairs as fast as she could.

"Good news?" she asked.

"Good news," said Minh. "She's OK."

Grandma Rose closed her eyes. "Thank God," she said. "I was more **horrified** than I wanted to show."

The next day, Minh had a video call with his grandmother in the hospital. She looked small and tired in the bed, with a bandage on her head. But her eyes were the same. "Why are you crying?" she said. "I'm not dead yet. Don't waste your tears."

Minh laughed. "I'm crying because I'm **thankful**. I'm so happy you're OK."

"I'm happy too," she said. "But I'm **irritated** with the doctors. They won't let me eat anything good. Only rice soup. Every day, rice soup."

"You need to listen to them, Grandma."

"I'm eighty-four. I don't need to listen to anybody." Then she smiled. "Now tell me about this girl. Lan says she's a nurse. Is she nice?"

Minh introduced Hana on the call. Hana was nervous, but she spoke a little Vietnamese that Minh had taught her: "Xin chào bà." Grandma was **amused**. She laughed so much that the nurse came to check on her.

"She has a good heart," Grandma said to Minh in Vietnamese. "I can see it. Be good to her."

After the call, Minh felt a quiet **joy**. It was not the excited joy of a party. It was deeper. It was the **satisfaction** of knowing that the people he loved were safe. He felt satisfied, **thrilled** and **hopeful** all at once.

"You really **adore** your grandmother," said Hana.

"I **admire** her," said Minh. "She's the strongest person I know. She had a hard life. There was a lot of **sorrow**: the war, losing her husband, being poor. But she never asked anyone for **pity**. She just kept going."

"Like Grandma Rose," said Hana.

"Exactly like Grandma Rose."

That weekend, Minh studied harder than ever for his exams. He was **eager** to do well. Every time he felt tired, he thought about his grandmother in her hospital bed, eating her rice soup and telling the doctors what to do. It made him smile. It made him want to **cheer**. And it made him want to make her proud. He was not shocked anymore, not even surprised. He was just **impressed** by her, as always.
