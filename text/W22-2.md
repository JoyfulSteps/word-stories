# The Babysitter

In October, Ben's sister, Lucy, had a problem. Her baby, Oliver, was five months old. She had to go to the hospital for an appointment, and her usual **babysitter** was ill. Her husband was working. "Can you **babysit** for three hours?" she asked Hana. Hana said yes, and asked Minh to help.

Minh had never looked after a baby. "What do I do?" he asked.

"Keep him alive," said Hana. "And happy, if possible."

Lucy lived in a small house. It was very organized. Everything had a place. "I used to have a **nanny** for my first child," she said. "But now I'm a **homemaker**. Or a **housewife**, as my grandmother says. I hate that word."

"Do you have a **housekeeper**?" asked Minh.

Lucy laughed. "I wish! No, I do all the household jobs myself." She gave them a list: when to feed the baby, how to change his nappy, where the toys were. "And put him on his play **mat** after lunch. He loves it."

Then she left. Minh and Hana looked at the baby. The baby looked at them. Then he started to cry.

The first hour was hard. Oliver did not want his milk. He did not want his toys. He did not want anything. Hana walked around with him. Minh tried to make funny faces. Nothing worked.

"Maybe the TV?" said Minh. He looked for the **remote** control. He found it under the sofa. He turned on a children's program. Oliver stopped crying and stared at the screen.

"Ha!" said Minh. "Modern parenting."

But then the TV stopped. The **extension** cable was loose. Minh looked behind the TV. There was a mess of wires: a long **cord** for the lamp, an **adapter** for a foreign plug, and a phone charger. He had to **unplug** everything and start again. "Every **device** in this house is connected to one socket," he said. "That's dangerous."

While Minh fixed the wires, Hana fed Oliver. Finally, he fell asleep. They put him in his bed very quietly.

Then they noticed things that needed fixing. A kitchen cupboard door was broken. A picture had fallen off its **hook** on the wall. The gate in the garden had a broken **padlock** and a rusty **chain**. Minh found a small **kit** of tools in a drawer: a **spanner**, a **wrench**, a pair of **pliers** and some small screws. He fixed the cupboard. He hung the picture. "Every **tool** in this house is new," he said. "Nobody uses them."

"You're like Mr. Okafor," said Hana.

"I learned from my father," said Minh. "He could **mend** anything. Motorbikes, radios, chairs. He said: if you can replace it, you can also fix it."

He found a can of paint and a **paintbrush** in the garden shed, but decided not to start painting someone else's fence.

When Lucy came back, the baby was asleep and the house was quiet. She looked at the kitchen. "Did you fix my cupboard?"

"Sorry," said Minh. "I hope that's OK."

"OK? That's wonderful! I've been asking my husband to fix it for six months!" She laughed. "Can you come every week?"

She also told them she was trying to **declutter** the house. "Too many things," she said. "Baby things everywhere. I want to give away the things we don't need. And do more recycling." She gave Hana a bag of baby clothes for the hospital.

On the way home, they did some **errands** for Lucy: they picked up her **groceries** from the shop and dropped off a parcel at the post office. Hana was quiet. Then she said, "You'll be a good father one day."

Minh almost walked into a lamp post. "That's a long way in the future," he said.

"I know," said Hana. "I'm just saying."

At the flat, Sam was trying to **assemble** a new bookshelf. There were pieces of wood everywhere. "The instructions are in Swedish," he said. "And there are three screws missing."

"Don't worry," said Minh, picking up the screwdriver. "I'm an expert now."

Later, Mr. Okafor came up to check that they could install the shelf safely and maintain it. "And adjust the screws every year," he said. "Wood moves."

Minh fell asleep that night thinking about babies, broken cupboards, and the strange, good feeling of being useful.
