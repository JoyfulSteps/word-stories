# A Busy Morning

Every school day in Minh's house started the same way. At six o'clock, Mum came into the bedroom and opened the window. "Wake up! Get up!" she said. Minh did not want to get up. Lan did not want to get up. But Mum always won.

First, Minh went to the bathroom. He took his **toothbrush** and put on some **toothpaste**. He brushed his teeth, washed his face with soap and dried it on a towel. Then he took a comb and tried to make his hair look clean and neat. It never worked. His hair always stood up like a brush.

Then he went to the kitchen. Grandma was already there. She liked to cook breakfast for everybody. Usually it was rice or noodles, but sometimes it was bread with butter and cheese, because Dad liked it. Mum drank her coffee from her big glass. Grandma cut the bread with a big knife. Dad ate quickly from a bowl with a spoon. Lan played with her fork and her plate and did not eat.

"Eat your food, Lan," Mum said. "Stop playing."

"I'm not hungry," Lan said. "I want to play with my toy."

"Toys are for after school. Eat."

After breakfast, Minh put his things in his bag: his books, a pen, two pencils, some paper, his ball for football at lunch, and a bottle of water. Mum gave him some money for lunch. "Don't buy sweets with it," she said. Minh did not say anything.

"Bring your coat, it's cold," Mum said. "And close the window." Minh shut the window. Then the family went out. Dad took his key and opened the door. He drove the motorbike out of the house. Mum sat behind Dad with Lan between them. Minh walked to school with Tuan, because it was near.

On the way, there was a small shop that sold everything: cards, umbrellas, a cheap camera, paper for letters, tickets for the bus. Minh and Tuan always stopped to look. The old man in the shop let them stand and read the comic books. He did not make them buy anything. "Read, read," he said. "Reading is good for children."

At school, Minh liked to read, write and study. He did not like to sit still. In class, he listened, but he wanted to run outside. At lunch, the boys played football in the yard until the bell rang.

After school, Minh had to work. He helped Dad at the motorbike shop. He carried tools, gave Dad what he needed and cleaned the bikes with an old towel. Sometimes Dad let him use the tools himself. "One day you can make your own motorbike," Dad said.

At home, Minh had to finish his homework before dinner. Then he had to wash the dishes. Then he could watch TV. Then Mum said, "Bed!" and the day was finished.

"Every day is the same," Minh said to Grandma one evening.

Grandma took a photo from the shelf. It was an old photo of Grandpa as a young man, standing next to a ship. "Your grandfather said the same thing when he was a boy," she said. "Then he went to sea and saw the world. Wait and see. Your life will not always be the same."

Minh looked at the photo for a long time.
