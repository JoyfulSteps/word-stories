# New Looks

Leo's injury changed many things in the house. One of them was food. The therapist said Leo needed good **nutrition** to heal. "More vegetables, more protein, fewer chips," she said. "And take your **vitamins**. You've finished the **antibiotic** from the hospital, so now we help the body with good food."

So Anna changed the family's diet. No more pizza on Fridays. No more cake every day. "We all eat too much sugar," she said. "Tom, you too."

"I'm not **obese**," said Tom. "I'm just big-boned."

"The doctor said you're overweight," said Anna. "Your **figure** isn't what it was. That's the official number. Do you want me to show you the **figures**?"

Tom did not want to see the figures.

Mia was the opposite. She was very skinny, and the doctor said she was a little **underweight**. "You need to eat more," Anna told her. Mia ate more cake. "Not cake," said Anna.

Meanwhile, Leo was getting stronger. He went to the gym with his therapist and worked on his arms and his upper body, because he couldn't use his legs. After three months, he was very **muscular**. He looked in the mirror a lot. "I look **gorgeous**," he said. Mia laughed so much she almost fell off her chair.

Then Leo had an idea. "I'm going to get a tattoo," he said. "A football on my arm. Because I'll play again."

"Absolutely not," said Anna. "Not until you're eighteen."

Mia was also changing her look. Since she started her Saturday art classes in London, she had become very **stylish**. She wore **fashionable** clothes from second-hand shops, big earrings and black boots. One day she came home with a new haircut: short, with a straight **fringe**. "In America, they call that **bangs**," said Tom. Mia rolled her eyes. Before, she always wore her hair in a **ponytail**. Now she looked older, more like an artist.

"You **resemble** your grandmother when she was young," said Grandma Rose, looking at her. "I had the same haircut in 1963."

Minh, on the other hand, was not stylish at all. He wore the same jeans every day, and his hair was always a mess. Hana said he looked **scruffy**. "A nice kind of scruffy," she added. But Minh decided to try. He bought a new shirt and got a haircut. After the summer, he was **tanned** from walking in the sun. His **complexion** was darker and healthier. "You look good," said Hana, and Minh felt about ten feet tall.

But the new diet was not easy for everyone. Daniel's café was full of temptation. The smell of fresh bread and chocolate cake was everywhere. Some customers smoked outside the café. One old customer, a heavy smoker, coughed all the time. He had smoked a **cigarette** every hour for fifty years. "I'm **addicted**," he said honestly. "It's a terrible **addiction**. I've tried to stop twenty times. To smoke is stupid, I know."

Daniel did not serve **alcohol** at The Blue Cup, but on Friday nights, some customers came in from the pub next door, a little **drunk**. One night, a young man was so drunk that he fell asleep at a table. Minh and Daniel had to call his friend to take him home.

"Alcohol is a kind of **poison**," said Hana, when Minh told her. "In small amounts, it's OK. But too much is really **poisonous** for your liver."

Hana knew a lot about health. She also knew about **sickness** in **pregnancy**. Ben's sister was having a very difficult time, and Hana sometimes went to help her. "She feels sick every morning," Hana said. "It's normal, but it's hard."

Minh realized that everyone around him was thinking about their bodies and their health: Leo's knee, Tom's weight, Mia's style, Ben's sister's baby. "I think the most important thing is wellbeing," said Hana. "Not just the body. The mind too. Being happy."

"Are you happy?" Minh asked her.

She thought. "Right now? Yes."

"Me too," said Minh.
