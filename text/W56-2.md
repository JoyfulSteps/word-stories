# Small Lives

In October, Hana moved to a new job at the hospital: the **neonatal** unit, which cares for newborn babies who are sick or born too early. It was **momentous** for her. She had always wanted to work with babies.

She came on the podcast to talk about it.

"Some babies are born very small," she said. "Some weigh less than a kilogram. They need help breathing, eating, staying warm. We care for them day and night."

"Is it hard?" asked Minh.

"Very. But also beautiful. Every day a baby gets stronger, it's a victory." She talked about the **tenacity** of tiny babies. "They fight. They want to live. It's amazing to watch."

She talked about medical advances. "**Genomic** testing can now find some diseases before a baby is born. And we understand **microorganisms** better: how bacteria can be dangerous for small babies, but also how good bacteria help them grow."

She described a baby she had cared for: born at twenty-five weeks, weighing seven hundred grams. "Her parents were terrified," she said. "Their **apprehension** was huge. We spent three months together. Now she's at home, healthy." The parents had written to thank her. "Our daughter's life is **unmatched** by any gift," they wrote.

Minh was proud of her. "You're amazing," he said off the microphone.

"I'm just doing my job," said Hana.

There were other stories that month.

The town bought a **derelict** building: an old school, empty for twenty years. Its **refurbishment** would create a centre for **fledgling** businesses: small new companies. "It will be a home for new ideas," said the mayor. Daniel helped plan it.

Leo's river trust bought fifty **hectares** of land by the river to turn into a nature reserve. "It's the biggest project we've done," he said. "Every hectare counts."

At the museum, the experts working on the codex found a page written in a secret code: a cipher. "The priest hid something," said Helen Ward. "We're trying to decode it." It became the town's favourite mystery. People sent ideas to the Gazette. A schoolboy cracked part of it. It was a recipe for beer.

The museum also received a gift: a beautiful **etching** of the town from 1750. The museum's **custodian**, an old man who had cared for the collection for forty years, was delighted. "History keeps coming home," he said.

Lan joined the podcast to talk about language. She explained how children build their **lexicon**: the words they know. "A three-year-old knows about a thousand words," she said. "Rose Mai probably knows more, because she has three languages." She also talked about **metaphorical** language: "time flies," "a heart of gold." "Children learn metaphors around age five. Before that, they take everything literally."

Rose Mai, aged three, was listening. "Time doesn't fly," she said. "Time has no wings." Everyone laughed. Lan said it proved her point.

On the podcast, Minh spoke **conversational** Vietnamese with a guest for the first time: an old woman from Hanoi who had lived in England for fifty years. They talked about Vietnam during the war, about **subsistence** farming, growing just enough food to survive, about her journey to England. Minh translated for listeners. It was one of his favourite episodes.

Helen Ward talked about history: the **hegemony** of the British Empire in the 1800s, and its decline. "No country stays on top forever," she said. She also talked about the **eradication** of smallpox in 1980, "one of humanity's greatest achievements," through **multilateral** cooperation between many countries.

There was some **commotion** at a council meeting when a man shouted that immigrants were taking jobs. Others shouted back. The meeting stopped. Minh reported it calmly. He also reported the facts: unemployment in Riverford was the lowest in twenty years. "Some people **loathe** change," wrote one reader. "But facts don't change."

A police officer was investigated for trying to **incite** violence on social media. He was suspended. "**Ethnically** motivated hatred has no place in the police," said the chief.

Minh noticed a **mismatch** between what people said in surveys and what they did. They said they wanted local shops, but they bought online. He wrote about it **convincingly**. Many readers recognised themselves.

There was a small health scare: Grandma Rose had a fall, but not serious. She was **gravely** annoyed more than hurt. "I'm ninety-three," she said. "I'm allowed to fall. I'm not allowed to stay down."

Her **unwillingness** to give up was, Minh thought, the most **observable** thing about her. Even now.
