# Little Minh

Before England, before college, Minh was a small boy in a small town in Vietnam. This is where his story starts.

When Minh was six, his teacher asked the children in his class, "Who is in your family?" Minh stood up. He was a thin boy with black hair, big eyes and a big smile.

"My name is Nguyen Van Minh," he said. "Nguyen is my surname. In Vietnam, the surname comes first. I live with my mother, my father, my little sister, and my grandmother."

His father and mother met at a wedding and were married when they were young. Mum was a good wife, and Dad was a good husband. They were good parents too, and Minh never doubted that each parent loved him. His father was a quiet man with strong arms and hands. He fixed motorbikes all day. His mother was a teacher at the girls' school. In English, some people say mom, some say mum, and some say mother. Minh just said "Mẹ." And for his father, "Bố." Dad.

Minh's mother loved her son and her daughter the same. Every night she gave them a hug and a kiss on the head. The baby of the family was Lan, Minh's sister. When she was born, she was very small, with no teeth and very little hair. Minh looked at her face and her tiny fingers. "She is so small," he said. "Is she really a person?"

"Yes," his grandmother laughed. "She is a person, and you are her big brother now. A brother takes care of his sister."

His grandmother lived with them. Her husband, Minh's grandfather, died before Minh was born. Grandma had a soft face, white hair and very old skin. She had only a few teeth left, but she ate everything. Every evening she told stories about men and women from long ago, about kings and dragons. Minh sat on the floor with his back against her knee and listened.

On Minh's seventh birthday, many people came to visit. His aunt and uncle came from the city with his cousin, Hoa. Hoa was a girl of the same age, and she was his best friend. They played in the yard all day and fell asleep on the same bed.

At dinner, Grandpa's old friend, Mr. Ba, came too. He was the oldest man Minh knew. "How old are you now, Minh?" he asked.

"Seven," Minh said.

"Seven! A good age. Let me look at you." Mr. Ba held Minh's face in his hands. He looked at his eyes, his nose, his ears and his mouth. "You have your grandfather's face," he said. "And his big ears!" Everybody laughed. Minh put out his tongue, and his mother hit his leg softly. "Be polite," she said.

Later, Minh had a pain in his stomach because he ate too much cake. His mother put her hand on his stomach and sang an old song. Her lips were close to his ear. Slowly the pain went away.

"Mum, do grandparents love their children's children more than their own sons and daughters?" Minh asked.

His mother smiled. "A grandparent's heart is very big," she said. "There is space for everybody."

Minh closed his eyes. His small body was tired. His feet were cold, his neck was warm, and his heart was full. He did not know that, one day, he was going to live very far away from this family he loved.
