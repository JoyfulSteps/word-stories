# The Film Premiere

In April, Mia and Sam had a big project. They had made a short film together. Mia painted the backgrounds and Sam did the animation. It was about a girl who could walk into paintings. The **plot** was simple: the girl walks into a famous **masterpiece** and meets the painter.

They entered it in a student film festival in London. It was chosen for the final. There was a **premiere**, the first public showing, at a real cinema. The whole family went.

Before the premiere, Minh interviewed them for the Gazette. "How did you start?" he asked.

"With a **sketch**," said Mia. She showed him a drawing in her notebook. "Just a girl and a painting. Then I made a **watercolor**. Then a big painting on **canvas**."

"Then I wrote the **script**," said Sam. "Well, the **screenplay**. It's the same thing for a film. Then we found the **cast**: the actors who did the voices."

The **cast** was small: Mia's friend from art school, an old actor from the Riverford theater, and Leo, who did the voice of a dog. "It's my best role," said Leo.

"What **genre** is it?" asked Minh. "Comedy? **Drama**? **Tragedy**?"

"A bit of everything," said Mia. "It's funny and **dramatic**. There's a **tragic** moment in the middle, when the girl almost can't get out of the painting. But it ends happily."

They showed Minh the **trailer**, a one-minute version with music. It was beautiful. "It looks like a **blockbuster**," he said.

"It cost three hundred pounds," said Sam. "So, not quite."

On the night of the premiere, the cinema was full. There were ten films in the final. Mia and Sam's film was number seven. The family sat together. Grandma Rose had her best hat. Margaret came from Scotland again.

The films were very different. One was abstract, just colors and shapes. One was very **realistic**, about a family in a poor city. One was a foreign film with **subtitles**. Another was **dubbed** into English, with voices that didn't match the mouths. One was by a young **comedian** who made the whole room laugh.

Then it was their film. The room went dark. The music began, a single soft piano **chord**. On the screen, a girl stood in front of a painting. Then she stepped into it.

For eight minutes, nobody moved. The colors were amazing. The animation was smooth. Leo's dog voice made everyone laugh. In the dramatic moment, when the girl was trapped, Grandma Rose held Margaret's hand. At the end, when the girl came out of the painting with a gift from the painter, there was silence.

Then the **applause** began. It was loud and long. People **applauded** for a full minute. Some people stood up. Someone shouted, "**Encore**!" even though it was a film and they couldn't play it again. Mia was crying. Sam looked like he couldn't breathe.

They didn't win first prize. They won second. But a **curator** from a big art gallery talked to Mia afterwards. "I'd like to **exhibit** some of your paintings from the film," she said. "We could **display** them next to the film, in a small exhibition."

A famous **playwright**, who wrote plays for the theater, talked to Sam. "Your story is lovely," he said. "Have you thought about a **sequel**?"

At the party after, a **sculptor** talked about art with Grandma Rose. A **craftsman** who made film sets talked with Leo about dogs. A well-known **entertainer** from TV asked for a photo with Mia. "Your **artwork** is beautiful," he said.

On the train home, Mia slept on Anna's shoulder. Sam looked out of the window, smiling. Minh wrote his article on his phone. He called it "Two Riverford Artists Step Into the Spotlight." He wanted to capture everything: the dark cinema, the bright screen, the sound of the applause.

"Do you think you'll make more films?" Minh asked Sam.

"Definitely," said Sam. "Next time, a full-length film. With a proper budget."

"Three hundred and fifty pounds?"

"At least."
