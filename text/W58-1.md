# A Home of Your Own

In January, the Gazette published a special report on **homeownership**. House prices in Riverford had risen **alarmingly**: three times faster than wages in ten years. Young people couldn't buy. Many rented forever.

The report showed a clear pattern. "There's a new social **stratum**," said Professor Ahmed. "People who own homes, mostly older, and people who rent, mostly younger. The gap is growing."

Minh interviewed young families. A teacher and a nurse, both thirty, had been saving for eight years. "We do everything right," said the teacher. "We work hard, we save. And still, prices outpace us every year."

The report also found something worse: a property **swindle**. A company had sold "investment flats" to older people, promising high returns. The flats were never built. The company disappeared with the money. Ten local families lost their savings.

Minh's team traced the company's owners. It took months of **rigour**: following money through five countries. In the end, police arrested two men. "Your reporting helped," said Inspector Shah.

The Gazette's editorial said: "A home is not a luxury. It's the foundation that **underpins** a decent life. Every person has an **inalienable** right to safety and shelter."

There were other stories that month.

The council launched a plan to build cheap homes for young people. The plan would **supersede** an older, weaker plan. "We have a **fallback** if money runs out," said the mayor. "But we hope we won't need it."

There was an **upsurge** in **veganism** in the town. Three vegan restaurants opened in a year. Daniel added vegan cakes. "Business follows people," he said.

Hana talked about **inoculation**: vaccines. "Vaccines are one of medicine's greatest successes," she said. "**Industrialised** countries have almost eliminated many childhood diseases. But we must keep vaccinating, or they come back."

Lan talked about English grammar on the podcast: the past **participle**. "'Eaten,' 'written,' 'gone.' Learners find them hard because many are irregular. Learn them in sentences, not lists." She also talked about English as a lingua **franca**.

The economy had an **upswing**: more jobs, more businesses. "Riverford is a **launchpad** for small companies now," said the business centre director. "Many start here and grow."

Ffion talked about the **interdependence** of nature: how every species depends on others. "Bees need flowers, flowers need bees. Fish need clean water, clean water needs trees. Everything is connected."

Leo, at the river trust, found **impurity** in a water sample: a small leak from an old pipe. It was fixed quickly. "We test every week," he said. "Better safe than sorry."

Helen Ward talked about history: a king who was **deposed** in 1399, removed from power by his own nobles. "Power is never permanent," she said. She also explained a **typology** of historical revolutions: some violent, some peaceful.

A local man was described as an **exemplar** of community spirit: he had cleaned the town's streets every morning for twenty years, unpaid. The council gave him an award. He was embarrassed. "I just like a clean town," he said. "Don't **overstate** it."

A **domineering** landlord who threatened tenants was taken to court. He lost. "Tenants have rights," said the judge. The ruling was a causative factor in a new council rule on landlords.

Grandma Rose's health was fragile. She had periods of **disorientation**, confused about the time or the year. Sometimes she thought Minh was nineteen again, just arrived. "Are you eating enough?" she'd ask. "You're so thin." Then her mind would clear, and she'd laugh. "Oh, I'm being silly."

"You're not silly, Grandma," said Minh. "You're just ninety-five."

"Exactly," she said. "Same thing."

The university announced a new **matriculation** record: more students from Riverford than ever before, many from the Sunday club. **Proportionately**, the town now sent more young people to university than any other in the county. Minh was proud. He had helped, in a small way.
