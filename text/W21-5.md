# The New Flat

Moving day was in September. The Parkers came with Tom's car and a borrowed van. They brought everything Minh owned: two suitcases, a box of books, his guitar, and a lot of things from the Parkers' **loft** that Anna said he "might need."

"I don't need a **hammock**," said Minh.

"Everybody needs a hammock," said Tom. "You can put it in the garden."

"The garden is the size of a table."

"Then you can put it in the living room."

Mr. Okafor met them on the **driveway** and helped carry the boxes up the stairs. He held the **banister** carefully. "My knees are not what they were," he said. Leo, who was walking normally again, said, "Tell me about it."

The flat was small but nice. There was a living room, a kitchen, a bathroom and two bedrooms. The kitchen had new white **tiles** on the wall and a new **dryer** for clothes. Every **appliance** was new: the fridge, the cooker, the washing machine. "Mr. Okafor did a good job," said Anna, checking everything.

But some things were old. The **plumbing** made a strange noise at night. The **heating** was slow. The old **boiler** in the bathroom took ten minutes to make hot water. "Is the **insulation** good?" asked Tom, knocking on the walls. "It will be cold in winter."

"Here's the **thermostat**," said Mr. Okafor. "Don't put it too high. Heating is expensive."

Minh's bedroom had old **wallpaper** with small yellow flowers. It was a little old-fashioned, but he liked it. There was a wooden bed with a high **headboard**, a **dresser** with drawers for his clothes, and a small desk by the window.

Anna and Mia made the bed. Anna brought a new **pillowcase**, sheets and a warm **quilt**. Mia brought a colorful **bedspread** that she had painted herself. "Now it's your room," she said.

In the living room, there was an old green sofa, a **recliner** that went back when you pulled a handle, and a **footstool**. "That's mine," said Sam immediately, sitting in the recliner. "I'm the one with a real job."

There was also a big wooden **sideboard** for plates and glasses. Above the table, there was an old glass **chandelier**. "It's very fancy," said Mia. One of the bulbs didn't work, and the **lampshade** on the floor lamp was broken, but they didn't care.

Grandma Rose brought presents. She gave Minh a small china **ornament** of a bird. "It sat on my windowsill for forty years," she said. "It will bring you luck." She also brought a new **doormat** for the front door. It said "WELCOME" in big letters.

At the back of the house, in the tiny garden, there was an old wooden **shed** and a small glass **greenhouse**. "You can grow tomatoes!" said Grandma Rose. "And herbs! A greenhouse is the best thing in the world."

When everything was finished, they all sat in the living room and ate pizza from the boxes. It was crowded. Leo sat on the floor. Mia sat on the footstool. Grandma Rose got the recliner, and Sam had to give it up.

"It's a good flat," said Tom. "Cozy. Warm. Well, it will be warm when the boiler works."

"Thank you," said Minh. "For everything. For two years. For being my family."

Nobody said anything for a moment. Anna wiped her eyes. Tom cleared his throat. "Come home every Sunday," he said. "For lunch. That's not a request."

"I promise," said Minh.

After they left, the flat was very quiet. Sam went to his room to work. Minh stood at the window and looked at the city lights. He felt happy, a little sad, and very grown-up.

Then his phone buzzed. A message from Grandma Rose: "Did you remember to hang the hammock?"

He laughed. Then he went to find the hammock.
